## ABOUT US

1000 Islands Travel was born from a fascination with places that are still difficult to reach.

For us, Indonesia has never been Bali, beach clubs and familiar tourist routes.

For many years we have travelled through the eastern part of the archipelago — by boat, small aircraft, ferry, motorcycle and sometimes simply on foot — looking for places where nature still remains stronger than people.

Places without crowds, queues or carefully prepared Instagram viewpoints.

Remote islands. Volcanoes. Coral reefs. Small fishing villages. Traditional communities. Wildlife. And thousands of kilometres of ocean separating one world from another.

We know many of these places personally. We know how to reach them, where to stay, which boats to use and, perhaps more importantly, what can realistically be done there.

Today we use this experience to create private journeys through some of the most remote parts of Indonesia.

# PART 2 — UI/UX REVIEW

## 1. The main issue is not the quality of the copy

The current copy has a clear tone and a strong central idea:

> Remote Indonesia. Off the Map.

The problem is that the same idea is repeated too many times in different forms.

Across the current text, the visitor repeatedly hears:

- remote Indonesia;
- difficult logistics;
- private journeys;
- local knowledge;
- boats and transfers;
- places beyond normal tourism;
- flexibility;
- limited infrastructure.

These are all important messages, but they should be distributed across the user journey rather than restated in almost every section.

The website should move from **repeating the positioning** to **proving it**.

---

## 2. The homepage currently tries to explain the whole business

This is the biggest UX problem.

A homepage should not contain the entire company story, every destination, every travel style, every operational detail, the complete B2B offer, FAQ and contact information in one continuous narrative.

Its job is to answer, quickly:

1. What is this?
2. Why is it different?
3. Where can I go?
4. What kind of journeys can I book?
5. Why should I trust this company?
6. What should I do next?

Everything else can live one click deeper.

---

## 3. The current content mixes four different navigation systems

At the moment the copy combines:

### A. Destinations
Raja Ampat, Misool, Triton Bay, Halmahera, Sangihe & Siau, Widi Islands.

### B. Travel formats
Private expeditions, private yacht journeys.

### C. Interests / experiences
Diving, wildlife, volcanoes.

### D. Company / trust information
How we travel, why travel with us, travel with confidence.

These should not appear as one flat list.

The visitor should immediately understand the difference between:

- **Where can I go?**
- **What can I do?**
- **How can I travel?**
- **Why should I use you?**

That distinction needs to be reflected in the menu, cards and page hierarchy.

---

## 4. The site currently sells geography better than it sells products

The destination copy is attractive, but the customer cannot yet see enough actual journeys.

For example, a visitor should be able to discover products such as:

- 8–10 Days Private Raja Ampat
- Raja Ampat + Misool
- Raja Ampat by Private Phinisi
- Triton Bay & Kaimana
- Halmahera Expedition
- Volcanoes of North Maluku
- Sangihe & Siau
- Banda Sea by Private Yacht

They do not need to be rigid packages.

They can be **sample journeys**.

This solves an important UX problem: people usually need something concrete before they can imagine a custom trip.

The ideal model is:

> Here is a journey you can understand.  
> Then we tailor it to you.

That is easier to buy than:

> Everything is custom. Tell us what you want.

---

## 5. The site needs two clear visitor paths

The company is serving two distinct audiences:

### Private travellers

They want inspiration, reassurance, destinations, itineraries, accommodation, logistics and a way to enquire.

### Travel professionals

They want operational capability, reliability, white-label support, response time, DMC services, supplier access, contracting and ground handling.

These audiences should share the same brand but have separate conversion funnels.

The homepage can introduce both, but B2B should have a dedicated landing environment.

# PART 8 — UX PRINCIPLES FOR THE DESIGN

## 1. Show first, explain second

This business sells places that most visitors cannot easily imagine.

Photography and maps are therefore not decoration. They are part of the product explanation.

Every major page should contain strong visual evidence.

---

## 2. Do not put large paragraphs over images

The copy is atmospheric, but premium travel websites become difficult to use when every section is a full-screen image plus a long paragraph.

Recommended rhythm:

- strong image;
- short headline;
- 40–90 words;
- CTA;
- next section.

Long-form copy should appear deeper in the page where the visitor has already shown interest.

---

## 3. Use maps

Remote geography is part of the value proposition.

A visitor may know Raja Ampat but have no idea where Kaimana, Sangihe or Halmahera are.

Maps can make the brand instantly more understandable.

Useful map layers:

- Indonesia overview;
- highlighted regions;
- sample route maps;
- yacht routes;
- combination journey maps.

---

## 4. Explain remoteness without making the product feel uncomfortable

The existing copy repeatedly talks about:

- no infrastructure;
- ferries;
- difficult logistics;
- remote accommodation;
- weather changes;
- limited facilities.

This is true and important.

But if overused, it can unintentionally make the product feel like hardship travel.

The website needs to maintain a balance:

**Remote, but managed.**  
**Adventurous, but organised.**  
**Authentic, but not chaotic.**

The operational difficulty should make the company more valuable, not make the trip sound unnecessarily stressful.

---

## 5. Premium does not have to mean "luxury everywhere"

One of the best ideas in the current copy is:

> We do not pretend that every remote destination is luxurious.

Keep this philosophy.

But visually and verbally distinguish:

**premium service** from **luxury infrastructure**.

The company can provide:

- excellent planning;
- private logistics;
- quality guides;
- selected boats;
- seamless coordination;
- responsive support;

even when the destination itself only offers simple accommodation.

That distinction should become part of the brand.

---

## 6. Avoid excessive "custom travel" language

Almost every premium travel company says:

- tailor-made;
- bespoke;
- custom;
- designed around you.

If repeated too often, this becomes generic.

The stronger differentiator here is not customization.

It is:

**We know how to operate in remote Indonesia.**

Customization should support the positioning, not replace it.

---

## 7. Use progressive disclosure

Do not show everything at once.

Example:

### Card

**Triton Bay**  
Whale sharks, limestone coastlines and remote reefs.

**Explore →**

### Destination page

Then explain:

- why go;
- logistics;
- accommodation;
- marine life;
- best season;
- sample itinerary.

This keeps the interface clean while preserving all the rich copy.

---

## 8. Separate inspiration from logistics

Travel pages should first create desire, then answer practical questions.

Recommended order:

1. Why go
2. What you will experience
3. Images
4. Sample journey
5. Accommodation
6. Logistics
7. Season
8. FAQ
9. Enquiry

Starting immediately with transport limitations and operational complexity weakens emotional conversion.

---

# PART 9 — WHAT IS MISSING FROM THE CURRENT CONTENT

The current copy is a good brand foundation, but the website still needs additional content before it becomes a strong commercial product.

## Essential additions

### 1. Actual sample journeys

This is the biggest missing layer.

### 2. Real photography

Prefer guest / expedition / team imagery over generic stock whenever possible.

### 3. Trust evidence

Reviews, real journeys, real people, operational examples.

### 4. Team

Who is behind 1000 Islands Travel?

Premium clients and B2B partners will want to know.

### 5. Practical destination information

Seasonality, access, duration, accommodation level.

### 6. Maps

Especially important for remote Indonesia.

### 7. Commercial orientation

Even if prices are not public, visitors need a sense of:

- typical journey duration;
- private vs shared;
- style of accommodation;
- vessel level;
- whether the company serves mid-market, premium or ultra-luxury clients.

### 8. B2B operational proof

For travel professionals, the website should demonstrate not only inspiration but execution.

The next copy-editing stage should not rewrite everything from zero.

- B2B
- Conversion

Remove duplicate statements between sections.

# FINAL RECOMMENDATION

The existing copy should be treated as a **content library**, not as the final order of a webpage.

The brand proposition is already reasonably clear:

> **Remote Indonesia. Off the Map.**

The next UX step is to reorganise the material around user questions:

### What can I book?
**Journeys**

### Where can I go?
**Destinations**

### How can I travel?
**Yachts / Experiences**

### Why should I trust you?
**About / Why Us / Reviews / Proof**

### I am a travel professional. What can you do for me?
**For Travel Professionals**

### How do I start?
**Plan Your Journey**

That change alone will make the site feel substantially clearer, more premium and more commercially useful without requiring the brand voice to be reinvented.
