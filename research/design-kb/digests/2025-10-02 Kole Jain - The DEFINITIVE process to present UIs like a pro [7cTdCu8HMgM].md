## Core thesis
A design looks better when it is presented, and the presentation should match the setting: creative showcase techniques (backgrounds, glow, exploded or skewed frames, collages) for portfolio and social; realistic mockups and working prototypes for client meetings, where the goal is to build confidence in the final product rather than to wow.

## Claims and advice
- [P] Two settings, two goals: portfolio/social = impress; client meeting = build confidence by showing the work in a clear, realistic context. (0:00, 4:03) — opinion
- [VIS-C] Plain background color is the most basic option; for a light-mode design, take the accent color, darken it and desaturate it (slightly grey) so the background does not outshine the design; add a faint shadow. (0:00) — practice
- [VIS-C][VIS-M] Dark mode: add a subtle glow by placing large blurred circles of the accent color behind the UI (soft ambient light); or a glassy background with a soft gradient stroke on the edges for depth. (0:00-1:00) — practice
- [P] Do not underestimate minimalism: bland background colors work because the design pops; "tough to go wrong" for nearly any design. (1:00) — opinion
- [VIS-L] "Exploding the image": let the design run off the screen edge (extend lines, patterns, a slider, or brand identity with added metrics) rather than crop it cleanly; clearly impractical but visually interesting. (1:00-2:01) — practice
- [VIS-L] Skew and pop-out: skew slightly (his numbers: 2 degrees vertical, -14 degrees horizontal), offset the dashboard and add a shadow; a grey placeholder where it used to be can accentuate the pop-out. Do not skew too much. (2:01) — practice
- [VIS-L] For multiple screens: stacking is boring; offset and slide screens off the page, then build a collage that avoids a rigid grid, rotate a few degrees, skew all, pop out the most important frame (e.g. hero section). (2:01-3:03) — practice
- [VIS-L][Q] The design itself must be large in these showcases, so landing pages work best; dashboards are too fine, so zoom into a specific section and animate it a bit. (3:03) — practice
- [VIS-I] Images are hardest to balance with the UI: must be relevant but not distract; better when there is a physical product (coffee shop, car); fluid gradient images suit software; a portfolio template is cited as integrating images well. (3:03-4:03) — opinion
- [CL][P] Mockups immerse the client: even a plain computer frame helps; a lifestyle mockup (iPad, Apple Watch in use) elevates it. (4:03) — practice
- [AI][AIS] "I don't think that AI is great at making thoughtful UIs, but it is good at making mockups." Method: generate a photo of a latest Apple product with a green screen using a ~30-word prompt (e.g. MacBook Air, green screen, orange faux-leather chair, background blurred, soft shadows, moody lighting, natural, lived in), key out the green and skew your design in: custom mockup in about 2 minutes. Details like concrete cylinders with cracks or a stained beige room sell the experience. (4:03-5:04) — practice, tool-dependent
- [Q][CL] High-quality product mockups matter a lot in e-commerce: "the difference between great and disastrous designs". (5:04) — opinion
- [CL][P] Prototype: "Don't describe, show." A visual mockup lets the user see, a working prototype lets them feel; reveals hidden features (swipe actions), modal animations, delete-sequence animations that static images cannot communicate. (5:04-6:05) — practice

## Techniques and steps
1. Pick the setting: showcase or client.
2. Showcase: plain muted background with faint shadow, or dark glow/glass; let the frame bleed off the edge or skew a few degrees; for several screens, make a loose offset collage; pop out the key screen.
3. Client: place the design in a realistic mockup (device in context); for custom mockups generate a green-screen device photo with AI and composite your UI.
4. Add a working prototype so small interactions can be felt.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Distinguishes portfolio flash from client confidence; against glossy showcase tricks in client meetings. States AI is weak for UI design but strong for mockups (contrarian relative to AI-design hype).

## For 1000 Islands
Limited: this is about presenting UI designs. Usable for our owner-facing deliveries: show the clickable prototype inside a realistic phone/laptop frame on a simple muted background, keep the presentation calm (client goal is confidence, not wow), and use a working prototype rather than descriptions (we already do). A muted background derived from the brand accent (darkened, desaturated) is a quick neutral for any board. AI-generated device mockups could make a polished one-pager of the site; showcase effects (skew, glow) are for a portfolio, not for the owner meeting.
