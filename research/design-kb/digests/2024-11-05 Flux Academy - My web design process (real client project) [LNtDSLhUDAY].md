## Core thesis
A strong website starts with an audit and a content structure, not with visuals. Audit brand, product and marketing; restructure content and write copy in a table-based "copy deck" that acts as the wireframe; build the visual system in parallel (type, palette, photography, one signature visual device); then design, get client feedback and build in Framer. The process is non-linear and holistic.

## Claims and advice
- [P] Every project starts with an audit of some level. Here three buckets: brand (decisions, likes/dislikes, admired brands), product (scope, constraints, how it works), marketing (every touchpoint: website, pitch, videos, brochures, one-pagers). (1:01-3:02) — practice
- [CL][P] In the brand audit, set ground rules with the client up front: what is untouchable, what needs convincing, what we definitely want to change. This lets you rationalize every suggestion instead of "trust us, it looks better". (2:01) — practice
- [P] Client goals shape the site: primary goal here was another funding round, secondary was new restaurant customers; two audiences, both need to be addressed. (1:01-3:02) — practice
- [REF][P] Screenshot competitors and adjacent companies for every section and communication point; organize screenshots into the structure where they belong. Used for layout and visual inspiration and for how others communicate. (4:03) — practice
- [P] Content restructuring: regroup repeated items (a benefit described three or five different ways), package consistently; result is a high-level content map/sitemap that "doesn't need to be anything formal". (4:03-5:05) — practice
- [P] Structural decision: merged homepage and product page into one, because a startup should first explain the product and not worry about looking like a one-product company; expand structure later as offerings grow. Said not to be a catch-all answer. (5:05-6:06) — opinion/practice
- [P][TOOLS] The copywriter writes in Google Docs using tables to organize sections (e.g. three benefits of consistent length), so the copy deck is effectively a wireframe; design starts after the copy is signed off. (6:06-7:07) — practice
- [P] "99% of the time" a company that asks for a new website actually needs a brand overhaul first, otherwise "putting lipstick on a pig" (caption garbled; verify wording). (7:07) — opinion
- [MB][P] Brand work was an evolution, not a redesign: kept logo and the two primary colors (dark navy, aqua), open on the rest. Visual goal: sophisticated tech leaders but approachable. (7:07-8:09) — practice
- [VIS-T] Font search for a sans that balances tech and friendly, usable in marketing and product UI; first choice was dropped because client wanted a free font, fallback (Aspekta, verify) used. (7:07-8:09) — practice
- [VIS-C] Palette: keep the original duo but tweak values to harmonize; create two extra versions of the aqua for contrast on dark and light backgrounds; accent colors inspired by fruit and vegetables seen in the machines; muted colors for diversity; use navy instead of black and reduced opacities for lighter shades (also white at reduced opacity) so text takes on the background color on white or beige; make several shades of beige. (8:09-9:09) — practice
- [VIS-I] Photography plan: identify the categories you can source from stock (here four: food prep and bowl assembly, restaurant team, close-up food) plus product photography. (8:09-9:09) — practice
- [VIS-I] Unify mixed-quality product images: isolate them on a beige background with an aqua gradient behind, later swap in higher-quality 3D renders. (9:09) — practice
- [VIS-I] A second image style: isolated floating fruits and vegetables with soft shadow to add depth in sections about ingredients. A quick icon style doc was also written. (9:09) — practice
- [EMO][VIS-L] A "visual anchor" derived from a core product feature (the modular machine fits any space) used across product marketing and website to tie everything together. (9:09-10:11) — practice
- [P] The process is not linear: mock up the site while the brand is still developing, then raise fidelity once the copy deck is done; iterate with client feedback (started dark, moved away from it). (10:11-11:11) — practice
- [TOOLS] Framer for the build: lets the designer spend time on micro-interactions, scroll and hover states, and lets later updates (new assets, a new video) happen directly in Framer without going back to Figma. (11:11) — practice, tool-dependent

## Techniques and steps
1. Audit brand, product, marketing; document what works and what does not, and look at how competitors/adjacent companies communicate.
2. Agree untouchable / negotiable / to-change items with the client.
3. Screenshot references per section; regroup existing content into a content map.
4. Copywriter builds a copy deck in tables = wireframe; get copy signed off.
5. In parallel: brand evolution (type, palette, photo categories, icon style, one visual anchor).
6. Mock up, iterate with client feedback, build in Framer; keep editing there.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against designing first: copy and structure precede visuals. Against redesigning only the site: advocates brand overhaul first. Cautions that merging product and company pages is not universal advice.

## For 1000 Islands
Useful for the process write-up: audit and ground rules with the owner ("untouchable / needs convincing / will change"), a content map before any design, copy written in a table as a wireframe, then a small visual system.
