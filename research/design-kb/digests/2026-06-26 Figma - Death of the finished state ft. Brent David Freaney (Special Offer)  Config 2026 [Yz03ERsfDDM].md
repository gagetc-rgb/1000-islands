## Core thesis
A designer's real work is collecting and consuming culture, not pushing pixels; rounds of design are exploration, not a staircase of ever-better versions; and the audience's reaction is the last step of the design process, so there is no "finished state". Trust between designer and client is the base of everything.

## Claims and advice
- [REF] He keeps a huge personal image library (about 2,000 images per year: Instagram, web, photos of street signs and ordinary things) and at the start of every project walks through it asking "is this it? no, no, yes". (19:36-20:37) — practice
- [REF][P] "Our real work is not in the actual design itself, but in the collecting of ideas": the work is experiencing and consuming media to the "craziest degree", not time at the computer getting the thing right. (20:37) — opinion
- [MB] For Brat he pulled anything that could belong to the "world of brat" into one Figma board: anything green, a Korean customs form, a dog's pet-store sign, green Martine Rose pants, a Whitney Houston water-bottle design he made earlier, system typography, Radiohead packaging, styrofoam, a Charli video, airport digital signs; he included stuff whether or not it made rational sense for his role. (21:38-25:42) — practice
- [MB] He builds the world (colour, material, tone, type-only album art that can be iconic) before the final cover, because vinyl packaging, merch and campaign needed a system. (21:38) — practice
- [CL][P] Contrary to the "show only your three best options" rule, he shows everything from a round, including what he dislikes, and uses the client's reactions to conclude "this is what I do like from this round". (23:39-24:41) — practice, contrarian
- [CL] "Design is a relationship of trust": without blind mutual trust it does not work; get it by showing work and references and explaining that you understand the situation and your perspective. (24:41) — opinion
- [P] Later rounds are not supposed to be better each time; much stays bad; sometimes a round is just exploring and learning what you do not want in order to find the boundaries. Rounds 1-2-3 "getting better and closer" is a misconception. (25:42-26:43) — opinion
- [VIS-T] You do not need to find "the right font": stick with five you like and know how to control; a font will not make the work by itself. Colour, weight, capitalisation, spacing, kerning, size and composition were all decided in turn; what "rings truest". (26:43-27:44) — practice
- [P] Brat brief was already very prescriptive from the artist (the word "brat", green, typographic); a specific directive lets you build a world around it. (21:38) — evidence
- [P][TOOLS] He designed Brat in Figma, then exported the cover at 12,000 px wide for vinyl print (12 in) rather than recreating it. (28:45) — evidence
- [EMO] The reuse of Brat by fans and brands (Ritz crackers, NATO, a subway line, per his words) turned the artwork into "an interface"; the audience's response is the last step of the process, "the audience is the medium". (29:51-31:52) — opinion/evidence from anecdote
- [P] "There is no finished state": even shipped and printed work remains a work in progress; he could iterate forever and only collaborators saying "we're done" got him out of tunnel vision. (31:52-33:53) — opinion
- [TOOLS] Opinion: designers should use a mouse, not a trackpad (21:38); use Figma however you like, even without auto layout (27:44). — opinion, low weight
- [CL] Design was an afterthought for him; he used it to help friends and collaborate with people already making things (logos for clothing lines, tour posters), 15 years in LA. (16:32-17:32) — evidence (autobiographical)

## Techniques and steps
1. Collect continuously: photograph signs, save anything you like to a library, tag by year.
2. At project start, filter the library against the project's personality; pull matches into one board, including odd ones.
3. Build the world first (colour, materials, type, tone) with the client; then do photography or final artwork.
4. Show whole rounds including bad work; read the reactions; narrow.
5. Pick a small stable set of fonts you control; decide caps, spacing, kerning, size, composition.
6. Export at needed print size; let the audience's response feed the next iteration.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against curating only the best options for the client; against the idea each round must improve; against the search for "the right font"; against the idea that design ends at delivery (audience as last stage). Contrasts with Bierut-style "present one strong option".

## For 1000 Islands
Useful as permission and method for Stage 2: build a personal reference library (photos, signs, hotel and boat details, light and colour) and filter it against the brand's "personality" at project start; show the owner a whole round of directions and learn from reactions rather than polishing a single option; and limit fonts to a small controllable set instead of hunting for a perfect typeface. The "audience is the last step" point supports shipping a minimal-scope site and iterating from real visitor reaction. Little else (album identity is not our domain).
