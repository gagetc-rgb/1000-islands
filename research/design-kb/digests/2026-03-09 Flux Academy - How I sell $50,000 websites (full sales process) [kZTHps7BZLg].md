## Core thesis
Treat a sales call as a medical consultation: diagnose before prescribing. Run a fixed sequence of calls instead of pitching everything and quoting a price on the first call, and make sure every call ends with a booked next step.

## Claims and advice
- [CL] His early mistake: presenting everything on one call, quoting price in the first meeting, and talking too much; people got bored. (0:00-1:00) - type: evidence (anecdote)
- [CL] First call is a consultation: focus on how you are different, not how you are the best; the client should talk about 80% of the time (an internal Flow Ninja metric). (1:00-2:01) - type: practice
- [CL] Before quoting, answer three questions: Why (why do they need the service; a client who only says "I want this" becomes the worst customer), Why now (timeline and urgency; without urgency deals stall or ghost), Why us (why this client should choose you; "it's obvious" is not an answer, e.g. 10 clients in their industry). (2:01-4:03) - type: practice
- [CL] If it is not a fit, say so and refer them elsewhere; the referral can later return as a bigger client and brand exposure. (3:02-4:03) - type: practice, anecdote
- [CL][P] Call 1 (demo/discovery) is about 25-30 minutes; no price, at most a ballpark. (4:03-5:05) - type: practice
- [CL][P] Call 2 is a strategy call: restate their problems, pain points, end goal and what success looks like; show relevant case studies; present your approach, including where you disagree with what they asked for. This is where you give free value, since AI means clients can do more alone and look for experts who guide. (4:03-5:05) - type: practice
- [CL] Price goes in the strategy deck, which is not shared before the call; sometimes price waits until the third call depending on complexity. (5:05-5:40) - type: practice
- [CL][P] Call 3 is the SOW call: walk through only the 3-5 most important contract sections (payment terms etc.), show the SOW is detailed, and only then email it. Call 4 is usually negotiation. (5:05-6:06) - type: practice
- [CL] Never end a call without booking the next one or agreeing the next touch point; get an anchor like "I'll come back Wednesday evening". Example cadence: Monday demo, Tuesday strategy, Wednesday SOW, then negotiation weeks or months; some sales ran two years. (6:06-7:07) - type: practice
- [BIZ] A defined sequence makes sales predictable (how many leads needed, how many close). (7:07) - type: opinion

## Techniques and steps
1. Call 1 (25-30 min): let the client talk; extract why, why now, why us; book call 2 before hanging up.
2. Call 2: strategy deck with their pain points, goals, case studies, approach; show pricing inside the deck, not before.
3. Call 3: SOW walkthrough on 3-5 key sections, then send it by email.
4. Call 4+: negotiation; always agree the next touch point.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against the common freelancer habit of pitching and quoting immediately; against "we are the best" positioning.

## For 1000 Islands
Light transfer: for our own client conversations, the why / why now / why us frame is a useful discovery checklist, and holding price until the approach is shown fits the offer-minimum, written sign-off pipeline. Heavy agency machinery (four calls, SOW negotiation) is overkill for our scope.
