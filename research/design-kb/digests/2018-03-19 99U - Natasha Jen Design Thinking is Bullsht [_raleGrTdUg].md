## Core thesis
"Design thinking" as popularised is a linear five-step diagram sold to non-designers, with no critique step and no visible outcome, so it cannot be judged. Real design work is messy, centred on tangible evidence (sketches, models, prototypes) and on crit; she challenges practitioners to show their outcomes for critique.

## Claims and advice
- [P] The popular process (Google image result, 2017): five hexagon steps, linear: empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test. She grants the steps sound reasonable. (1:04-2:07) — evidence
- [P][Q] Missing piece 1: critique ("crit"). In design school every idea is shown with evidence and everyone "critiques the heck out of it"; that is how you tell whether something is good, and the diagram has no such step. (2:07-3:08) — opinion
- [P][AIS] Missing piece 2: the whole discipline is reduced to one tool, the sticky note (3M Post-it); asks why a messy, tool-rich field ends in one medium. (3:08-4:09) — opinion
- [P][CL] Her definition of design thinking today: it packages designers' way of working for non-designers by codifying it into a prescriptive step-by-step problem-solving approach "that can be applied by anyone to any problem". (4:09) — opinion
- [P] Brief history: Herbert Simon (design as a way of thinking), Robert McKim (visual thinking, verify), Brian Lawson (architecture), Nigel Cross (education, verify), Peter Rowe (book "Design Thinking", Harvard), Rolf Faste (Stanford curriculum, verify), David Kelley/IDEO (business), Richard Buchanan (human concerns). (4:09-6:15) — evidence (self-described as "by no means in-depth")
- [P] A good example: OXO Good Grips peeler, shaped through extreme use cases (strong hands to arthritis); there is tangible evidence you can inspect to judge whether the thinking was good, and iterate. Idea of improvement is key. (6:15-7:16) — evidence
- [P][Q] Without an outcome you cannot critique how good a method is; the diagram shows no outcome. Other fields "opportunistically" latch on to design thinking. (7:16) — opinion
- [Q] Examples sold as design-thinking successes that she finds obvious: a GE children's MRI scanner with cartoon murals, Olay targeting women in their 30s and 40s after losing older customers, an IBM portal that looks like "a lot of photos I have seen in my life". (8:17-9:18) — opinion
- [P] Real designers use "hard and tangible words" to critique work; design-thinking words are corporate jargon, buzzwords, not real criticism. (9:18-10:18) — opinion
- [P][AIS] Design turned into a "box people want to check off". (10:18) — opinion
- [P] Counter-examples: Charles and Ray Eames (learning by doing, finding needs and constraints before designing, no Post-its), Steve Jobs (intuition, people's desires over business means), Pentagram's own messy office with a wall of past work for self-critique, OMA foam models as predecessors of the buildings. (10:18-12:22) — opinion/evidence
- [Q][P] "Real designers surround themselves with evidence"; evidence plus crit makes work better; messy environments, not hexagons. (12:22) — opinion

## Techniques and steps
Not a how-to. Implied practice: make something tangible early, put the work on a wall, show it for critique, iterate on evidence, and publish outcomes so others can critique them.

## Tools and resources
- OXO Good Grips peeler: positive case study of inclusive iteration (6:15)
- Pentagram's work wall and OMA foam models: examples of evidence-led studios (11:20)

## Quotes
- "Real designers surround themselves with evidence." (12:22, paraphrase)
- Her challenge: share the evidence and outcomes "and allow us to critique and comment on it" (12:22, paraphrase)

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against IDEO-style design thinking as a universal process; against hexagon diagrams and sticky-note workshops; for craft-centred, critique-driven practice. Counter-view she does not address: design thinking arguably helped non-designers value design at all.

## For 1000 Islands
Use the stages as scaffolding but keep crit: show the owner concrete artefacts (clickable wireframes, a moodboard) rather than diagrams of process, and judge them against clear terms ("does the hero photo show the boat day", "is the CTA reachable") instead of jargon. Keep a wall of reference and past versions to self-critique. Relevant to the AI-era risk that a prompt pipeline replaces critique.
