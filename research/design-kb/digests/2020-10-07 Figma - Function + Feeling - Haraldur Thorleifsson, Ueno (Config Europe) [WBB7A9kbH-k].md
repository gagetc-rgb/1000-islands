# Function + Feeling - Haraldur Thorleifsson, Ueno (Config Europe)

## Core thesis
Technology and digital products have too often been purely functional, treating people like the early psychologists treated infants (food and shelter only); people also need connection and belonging. Designers should combine function and feeling in everything and give their work a meaning beyond the craft.

## Claims and advice
- [EMO] Harlow monkey experiment as a metaphor: infant monkeys preferred a cloth surrogate mother (comfort, belonging, no food) to a wire one with milk; so humans have emotional needs beyond functional ones. (4:06-7:11) — evidence (historic experiment, used as analogy)
- [EMO][AIS] Much of the digital world is "purely functional" and its results are showing: we can get anything with a button but are not happier; we can reach anyone yet feel isolated and fractured. Technology can help people belong, not only deliver food and shelter. (7:11-9:12) — opinion
- [EMO] His own reason for obsessing over "just a website": for years he used making things as a way to connect with others, to help them enjoy a moment. (11:13-12:14) — opinion (personal)
- [EMO][Q] Case: Google Santa Tracker (he led a global team for four months; games, videos, silly fun things); used by millions in days; a viewer later told him it helped through a hard period. "It's just a website" is wrong when it makes people happy. (12:14-14:16) — evidence (anecdote)
- [EMO] Personal case: through Facebook he asked family and friends in Icelandic for photos, stories and especially a video of his mother; strangers sent hundreds of photos and two videos, one with her voice, the first time in 30 years; "none of this would have happened without social media". Technology can create real human moments of joy, love and processing grief. (16:22-20:28) — evidence (personal story)
- [EMO][DS] "Combine function and feeling in all of my work"; functional first so people can do what they want, then create emotion. Design systems are great but "just tools": why and what you build matters more. (22:29-24:31) — opinion
- [BIZ] Work needs meaning; otherwise it is a meaningless quest to create a perfect design system (a dig at tool-focused design culture, said at a Figma conference). (23:30-24:31) — opinion
- [BIZ] Close: master your craft but also "work on mastering your humanity". (24:31) — opinion

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against purely functional, data-driven product thinking; against treating design systems or tools as the point of the craft. Contrasts with his own 2019 Awwwards talk by dropping the jokes for a personal argument.

## For 1000 Islands
Light takeaway: a travel site sells feeling as well as logistics, so the photo-led experience should carry emotion first and function (booking, details) second but never be missing. Nothing actionable on layout or process. Also a tonal caution: this is a motivational talk and should not be used as evidence for design decisions.
