## Core thesis
In a world of goldfish attention, work that gets selected is work presented with care: story, annotated process, clean data and subtle mockups, all consistent with the project. Show the process, not just the final design, because clients now suspect that "typing a prompt" produced it.

## Claims and advice
- [Q][CL] Attention is scarce (he cites human attention dropping from 12 to 8 seconds, "probably even lower now", no source); first impression decides whether you land in the "maybe pile". (2:03-4:07) - type: opinion
- [CL][AI] Client distrust is rising because clients imagine design is "typing a prompt into a box", lowering perceived work; counter by showing process, annotations, paper sketches, explorations. Design as a process, not a single outcome and a Figma file. (11:15-12:17) - type: opinion
- [AIS] His 2026 view: design done right is among the last things AI replaces; learning design still makes sense, but it is about fundamentals, not AI tools or speed of systemizing in Figma. (1:02-2:03) - type: opinion
- [EMO][P] Case study storytelling: use a third-person narrator (consistent person across photos, own photos or stock) instead of a templated persona; place 5ish story beats evenly down the page. Vary the concept per project. (6:09-10:14) - type: practice
- [VIS-I][EMO] Gaze rule: first image of a person looks at camera (eye contact, emotional bond); later images look to the side or point at the text, because people follow gaze; he says heat maps show attention lands on the text then. (9:13-10:14) - type: practice, claimed evidence (heat-map studies unnamed)
- [VIS-L] Charts/tables: build with consistent spacing (e.g. 120 and 60 px squares as alignment units; 8 px between rows; 52/70 px row heights), shortened labels, left-aligned names and right-aligned numbers (he rejects centered data), tinted dark grey text instead of black, AA contrast on header fills. (13:18-46:49) - type: practice
- [VIS-C] Data styling should reuse the project palette; make chart lines thick (6 px), gradient fills with reduced opacity, annotate where something changes ("rapid growth") so the chart tells a story; highlight only the winning pie slice. (17:20-33:40) - type: practice
- [VIS-L] Do not show raw spreadsheet-looking data: it looks pasted from Excel and weakens the case study. (47:51) - type: opinion
- [P] Reverse-engineer the process for a portfolio: from final hi-fi screens make low-fi wireframes on paper (traced phone template, photo, desaturate, mask) and digital (strip images, shadows, icons; two font sizes; handwriting-style font such as Marker Felt; grey rectangles for body text). The bigger the contrast between wireframe and final, the better. (47:51-1:08:14) - type: practice
- [VIS-L] "13 degree rule": rotate mockup groups 12-15 degrees (he uses 13) because tilt makes alignment flaws harder to see and instantly looks better; leave one corner open, equal gaps everywhere. (1:10:14, 1:49:55) - type: opinion/practice
- [VIS-L] Mockup grids: distribute by visual weight (heavy photo screens next to light form screens, never login beside registration); stagger heights; keep identical spacing (50+ px with shadows); different screen per phone; avoid overlapping screens when starting out. (1:27:28-1:42:47) - type: practice
- [VIS-C][Q] Subtlety rule: best mockups are subtle; light/white backgrounds, at most one decorative element (e.g. one leaf), saturated or dark backgrounds only for dark-mode screens; "it's all about the screens, not the background". (1:45:50-1:55:00, 2:28:42) - type: opinion
- [VIS-L] Shadows: equal X and Y offsets (about 16-24), blur at least 2x (4x for minimal frames), low opacity, colored from the palette rather than black; larger/closer objects cast larger, stronger shadows to create depth. (1:45:50-2:27:40) - type: practice
- [VIS-L] Minimal frames: 16 px (up to 20-24) white bezel, inner radius matched to outer, "clay" look via two 4 px inner shadows; elements breaking out of the frame (an action sheet, a carrot) add depth, but only one or two per piece and fully cover the original underneath. (2:04-2:21:32) - type: practice
- [VIS-L] Align text and lines optically (e.g. a letter stem continuing a connector line); check radii, shadows and overlaps before export. (1:24-1:25, 2:23:35) - type: practice
- [CL] Quantity counts for juniors: recruiters want to see that you made many screens, so fill the frame. (2:09:18, 2:27:40) - type: opinion

## Techniques and steps
1. Pick a narrator and storyline; shoot or choose consistent photos; spread them and any data visuals evenly through the case study (6:09-9:13).
2. Chart: axes with two lines at 50% opacity, 120/60 px spacing squares, thick colored line, gradient fill, annotated turning point (13:18-23:27).
3. Table: colored header band, 8 px row gaps, big readable numbers right-aligned, dots on only the extreme values; minimal variant with 3-point brightness alternating rows (35:42-46:49).
4. Wireframes: sketch on paper with a phone template, photograph without shadows, desaturate, crop and mask; or downgrade the hi-fi digitally; add 13-degree flow annotations with dots, connectors and short labels (47:51-1:26:27).
5. Mockups: build a phone mask (rounded rectangle plus notch, subtract), mask each screen at the same width, fix top/bottom edges with blurred gradient patches, then lay out a staggered grid with equal gaps (1:27:28-1:42:47).
6. Finish: background export at 5x under the phones, shadow rectangles at 45 degrees, one decoration, rotate 13 degrees, final consistency check (1:44:48-2:24:37).

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against centered table numbers and against decorative or dark mockup backgrounds; against templated personas; says AI will not replace good designers while accepting that clients distrust AI-era work. Openly reverse-engineers the process (hi-fi first, wireframes made afterwards, 47:51 and 1:18:19, joking "I'm not going to tell if you're not going to tell"), which sits awkwardly with his own advice to show real process and counter client distrust.

## For 1000 Islands
Mostly low relevance (app UI portfolio presentation). Transferable: (1) when presenting Stage 2 directions to the owner, show process and annotation, not just finished screens, because AI-built work invites "you just prompted it" doubt; (2) keep presentation chrome quiet so the photography/design carries the pitch; (3) consistent spacing and optical alignment checks before showing anything; (4) the gaze rule (first photo eye contact, later people looking toward the key text or CTA) could apply to people photography on the site; (5) low-fi to hi-fi side-by-side is a ready format for the owner walk-through of grey v5 versus later stages.
