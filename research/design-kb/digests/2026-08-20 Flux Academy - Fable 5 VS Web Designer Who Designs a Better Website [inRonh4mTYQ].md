## Core thesis
Same brief, one shot for Claude with the impeccable skill vs 6 hours for a 20-year designer using references, generated imagery, premium fonts and AI-assisted development. The designer's own conclusion: AI avoided slop and had a sound process, but the human's research-driven concept, imagery and craft details came out stronger; the AI result felt "too old school like a 90s website".

## Techniques and steps
1. Read the brief as a problem type (persuasion vs portfolio).
2. Research local and global competitors; note what they all do (cliché) and the best outliers.
3. Name the trap: what would any AI/template produce; deliberately avoid it.
4. Find a feeling from an adjacent archetype and abstract it (colors, light, posture), not its literal subject.
5. Generate imagery and a hero video from the reference direction; iterate re-prompts until "clean".
6. Choose fonts (premium foundries) that carry a physical metaphor of the trade (sharp, technical).
7. Explore hero layouts in three color variations; choose; then each next section.
8. Hand the rest to the AI with explicit interaction direction (named library, motion idea).

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Counters the assumption that a skilled AI with a "no-slop" skill wins: human research, tooling (paid fonts) and concept still beat it, in his view. Concedes the AI avoided clichés through random direction picking, and that a human would also use AI to build faster. Conflict of interest: course-seller judging himself.

## For 1000 Islands
Strong template for Stage 2. Do competitor research first, name the "Thousand Islands cliché" (blue water, sunset, canoe photo) and avoid it by deliberate choice; pick an adjacent feeling and abstract it; generate multiple directions, then pick with owner; choose a font with a physical metaphor, keep brand voice quiet for an upper-middle audience; use AI for build under a human spec. Do not generate people for our site; this stunt used AI portraits of a real founder, which is not our case.
