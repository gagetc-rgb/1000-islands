## Core thesis
A 30-person cross-discipline team at Koto built Seasoned, a free branding-industry onboarding platform with four books, using a self-run branding process: brief, wide exploration, three routes, pick the least expected one, name, play with new tools, then ship with a digital partner. The lessons are that play, fun and time-taking pay off in the output.

## Claims and advice
- [P] The project started with a Slack call-out (20 June 2023), a team of about 30 (designers, writers, strategists, client services, motion and digital), a one-line brief ("make getting into the design industry feel less scary"), then a 4 p.m. Wednesday session split into three teams. (1:42-4:46) — practice, anecdote
- [P] Normally Koto teams are six to eight people over a few weeks; here they deliberately tested cross-discipline work at all levels. (3:44) — evidence
- [P][MB] Exploration: sketches, thoughts and references were grouped into categories; after a few weeks two strong routes remained (Route 1 "Koto U", Americana college aesthetic; Route 2 an alternative-youth-movement look). (3:44-5:46) — practice
- [MB][AIS] They worried that "cool graphic design" is plentiful and may itself be a barrier; a third, unexpected route (cookery-book and TV-cook visual language, "Delia Smith holding an egg") won. (5:46-6:49) — practice, opinion
- [EMO][MB] The chosen reference fit because it was true to culture (food as universal conversation, step-by-step tutorials with no barriers) and true to the agency (a giant blue kitchen table, egg-themed meeting rooms, bake-offs). (5:46-6:49) — practice
- [VIS-L][VIS-T] Borrowed cookery tropes: step-by-step breakdown, 1970s-80s food maximalism, kitchen sayings as copy. (6:49) — practice
- [P][CL] Self-initiated work still went to the CCO ("give us some money"); his reaction produced the working brief "Make it weirder". (7:51) — anecdote
- [EMO] Naming: "Seasoned" works as food word, shows their role (seasoned professionals imparting knowledge) and carries their optimism. (8:51) — practice
- [AI] Used AI image tools in June-July 2023 for experimentation with no client deadline; results were "ropy" with "unhappy accidents", but it taught them the technology. Look: wrong textures, "everything's not as it seems", prompting a second look. (8:51-9:52) — evidence, tool-dependent

- [VIS-M][P] Handed the "chaotic" designer output to digital and motion teams, who "make sense of it" and spread playful details through the experience (even a "come back soon" microinteraction). (11:53) — practice

## Techniques and steps
1. Team and brief first: a call-out, a diverse team, a one-line question as the brief (1:42-3:44).
2. Wide, fast exploration in small groups; gather references and sketches; cluster them (3:44-4:46).
3. Narrow to two or three routes; include one deliberately unexpected route (4:46-5:46).
4. Check each route against "true to culture" and "true to us" (5:46-6:49).
5. Get leader approval with working samples, not slides, and a brief that pushes ("make it weirder") (7:51).
6. Name it, then experiment with new tools and a custom typeface (8:51-10:53).
7. Hand off to digital and motion for details, then to a dev partner (11:53-12:57).

## For 1000 Islands
Low relevance. Two process echoes: (a) show two or three genuinely different routes, including one non-obvious reference from outside the category, and test each against "true to the place" and "true to the owner"; (b) keep direction plus real working samples for the sign-off. Their visual outcome (maximalist, playful) does not fit an upper-middle travel audience, and a custom typeface is out of scope.
