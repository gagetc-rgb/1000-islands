## Core thesis
A small studio wins by choosing clients carefully and refusing the wrong ones, keeping a strong consistent in-house design philosophy (graphic-design roots, grids, type, Swiss-influenced), and keeping the team learning through side projects, workshops and internal sharing channels. They treat clients as collaborators, polish past scope, and avoid growth for its own sake.

## Claims and advice
- [CL][BIZ] Pick the right clients; wait months for one rather than take a "no money, no fun" project. Their line: clients should want "OBYS", not "just a designer". (10:12-13:17) — practice
- [CL] When a client already wants the studio's style, there is no need to explain "beautiful grids and typography"; both sides are on the same page. (11:16, repeated 0:00) — opinion
- [CL][BIZ] Red-flag industries they refuse: politics, military, guns, alcohol, drugs (even marijuana). They say so honestly ("not close to our philosophy") instead of claiming they are overbooked; sometimes such rejected clients still recommend them. (17:27-18:30) — practice
- [CL] Red flags in client behavior: many meetings with no clear decision-maker, a top-down "dictator" everyone fears so approvals are uncertain, many decision-holders that "kill" a project, feedback like "I don't like it" without reasons (two weeks spent hunting what they like), clients who phone at 5 a.m. Sunday or demand all-nighters. (18:30-23:46, 25:49) — practice
- [CL] Best projects: honest two-way discussion; a client can say "I don't like this font" and explain why it matters for the business, and the studio finds a solution together. Trust includes pushing the client in design areas (fonts, typography). (21:40-22:44) — practice
- [CL][BIZ] Selectivity creates value: rejecting clients builds the appeal of the studio. For beginners: say no (politely) rather than get trapped for 2-3 months on a project you are not proud of; set simple values like "no alcohol, no drugs" even when money calls. (23:46-24:47) — opinion
- [CL][P] Their goal is not "site of the day" on every project but making each project as innovative as possible for its niche (even a political polls website), however boring the field. (13:17-14:21) — opinion
- [Q][CL] After launch they often ask the client for a few more hours to fix small things (copy, an image, 4 words not 5) and help with Instagram images so social matches the site. The host agrees: "we don't publish till we are happy 100%". Quality drives awards, awards bring more qualified clients. (14:21-16:23) — practice
- [VIS-L][VIS-T] "Web design is graphic design plus UX": same principles of typography, composition and color, then interactivity added. They keep margin systems, grid rules, font-size and contrast rules, and favorite fonts; their style is close to Swiss style, rooted in Bauhaus and Ukrainian avant-garde modernism. (12:17-13:17, 34:01-36:03) — opinion
- [EMO][REF] Inspiration comes from anywhere (film, ads, books, fashion). Their first Site of the Day was a 7-day tribute site to photographer Peter Lindbergh after his death, built with a site builder: sometimes you do not need to dream for years, just create something. (30:55-31:55) — evidence (their own story)
- [P][BIZ] Internal culture practices: "design for soul" every Friday producing side projects (typography principles, grids, a message-to-Ukraine project); a Slack-style channel of inspiring sites and identities; a channel called "how not to do" discussing why award winners or street ads work or do not; design digest/review; workshops where each person teaches the team (modernism, Webflow, English, management). (26:50-29:52) — practice
- [BIZ] Side projects help designers avoid burnout and, for beginners without dream clients, work on fictional dream-client projects to attract the kind of client you want; the portfolio defines who you attract (show political work and you get political clients). (27:50, 31:55-32:57, 54:32) — practice
- [BIZ] Growth: they considered growing the team but concluded they can "earn more and influence more" while small with the right people; planning a rebrand and own typeface (the art director is drawing all letters), their own website and an identity based on Ukrainian modernist roots. (37:06-38:07) — opinion
- [BIZ][CL] Freelancer/start-up advice: do not be shy or afraid (mistakes are normal); expect to be copywriter, manager, accountant; learn invoices and contracts early (they struggled with this). (50:28-55:33) — opinion
- [CL][BIZ] Over-deliver: with first clients especially, do more than quoted (they sometimes skip additional invoices for a good client). (55:33-57:38) — practice, with a business trade-off
- [P][CL] They do not include animation or prototype in quotes but do it in about 95% of cases: a quick Webflow prototype or simple animation of key sections makes clients say "wow", and the host adds a one-hour After Effects mock shows effects better than describing them and saves rework. (57:38-58:38) — practice
- [BIZ] Team: freelancers (3D, motion, WebGL) help when needed; they keep design in-house for control of style but outsource some development. (5:06) — evidence
- [P] Master class (Awwwards/Domestika/CGMA, 10 weeks, up to 20 experienced students) takes a "memorable UI" homepage from brief, moodboard, wireframes to Figma prototype and After Effects animation; the founder gives written feedback on each student's weekly homework (10 min each). (46:19-49:26) — evidence (describes their course; promotional)

## Techniques and steps
1. Screen clients before accepting: industry list, decision-maker structure, tone of feedback, respect for working hours. (17:27-23:46)
2. Say honest no, not "we are busy". (18:30)
3. Hold weekly side-project time and an internal inspiration channel plus a "how not to do" channel. (26:50-28:51)
4. Run internal workshops where team members teach each other. (28:51-29:52)
5. Before launch, polish copy and images; offer to align social imagery to the site. (14:21)
6. Show clients a rough animation or prototype of key sections to avoid misunderstanding. (57:38-58:38)

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against "every client is good, take the money": they refuse high-budget clients on values and fit. Against growth: they argue small is more profitable and influential. Note conflict with the Bierut talk: Bierut says never educate the client; OBYS prefer clients who already understand the style so no education is needed, a similar filter in other words.

## For 1000 Islands
Useful for how we run the owner relationship: (1) the "decision-maker clarity" red flag: confirm that the owner alone decides and that nobody behind him can veto sign-offs; (2) work with feedback that has reasons, ask "what business problem does this solve?" when the owner dislikes something; (3) a quick clickable or animated prototype of key sections before sign-off prevents misunderstanding (matches our Stage 1 clickable prototype); (4) polish copy and photos before launch; (5) but note their "over-deliver for free" habit conflicts with our scope-minimum rule: extras are paid, so adopt the attitude (care) but not free scope. (6) Internal "how not to do" analysis is a good habit for building our reference library. Their Swiss/Bauhaus-modernist style is not our direction; only the discipline of margins, grids, type and contrast rules transfers.
