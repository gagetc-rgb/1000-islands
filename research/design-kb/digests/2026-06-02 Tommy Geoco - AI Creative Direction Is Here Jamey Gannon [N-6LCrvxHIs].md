## Core thesis
AI is a normal tool that has to be learned, not magic: models are strong at small tasks and weak at big impossible changes, so a human creative director stays in the loop. Gannon's practical contribution is a client-facing moodboard method (like/dislike on references to build a shared language) and a five-principle framework for AI image generation: aim before you shoot, use image references over long prompts, nothing arrives finished, work in steps, use the right tool.

- [AI][AIS] "AI creative director framework", five principles: (1) aim before you shoot; (2) an image is worth a thousand words (use style references / SREFs, not long prompts; a vision LLM described one image in huge detail, showing the latent data is already in the image); (3) nothing good arrives finished (one-shot works ~50% of the time, ~80% with good aim and references; Photoshop fixes are fine); (4) work in steps (remove the background first, then insert the subject; remove label text, crop the label, then apply it); (5) use the right tool for the job (tiny logos added in Photoshop; Midjourney failed a "square peg in a round hole", Nano Banana succeeded). ## Techniques and steps
1. Aim before you shoot: decide what you are making and roughly how it should look, then set three inputs: model, reference material, prompt.
2. References for images come in three kinds: subject, style (SREF), and prompt text; prefer style references to long descriptions.
3. Client moodboard: collect a pool of images, let the client like/dislike with comments (FigJam stickers or a swipe tool), read the pattern, then design from the shared language.
4. Work in steps: strip the base image first, then add the new element in a second pass.
5. Expect breakage when combining multiple styles/elements; fix in Photoshop.
6. Vibe-code a tool: voice-dictate the brief, get a PRD, split P1/P2, feed P1 to Claude Code, then edit the design.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against both "AI will take everything" and "one-shot prompt does it all": both assume AI is perfect. Against long prompts in favor of image references. Against value-based branding prices for startups: if the market wants a fast cheap sprint, take it. Hot take: designers will split into product people and brand people.

## For 1000 Islands
The thumbs-up/thumbs-down moodboard method is directly usable with the owner: show a pool of 20-30 travel/hospitality images and have him mark likes and dislikes with a one-line comment; it guards against "make it like Apple" briefs and gives us a shared vocabulary. Reference images beat prompts for AI-assisted imagery. Caveat for a photo-led travel site: real photography of the real islands is the product; use AI for small edits (cleanup, background) rather than inventing places, in line with her view that AI is good at small stuff. Her "work in steps" and "nothing arrives finished" rules apply equally to our AI-assisted HTML builds.
