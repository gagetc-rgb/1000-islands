## Core thesis
The studio argues that in a sea of template-like sites, "digital character and the human touch are the ultimate luxuries": narrative-led design, a very small core team that keeps one vision from concept to launch, and a deliberately slow research-and-sketch phase. Its photography project Ottografie is the most relevant evidence of how to make a photo-led site feel like an environment rather than a grid.

## Claims and advice
- [P][AIS] Founding motive (2014): digital design "was becoming very template-like"; they wanted expressive, refined experiences instead of "systematic layouts". — opinion
- [P][BIZ] Team of three in one creative language (designer-led technical director joined as freelancer, partner from 2020): "nothing gets lost in the process", the final product "feels exactly like the original vision". Argument for a designer-minded developer to avoid design dilution. — practice (self-described)
- [BIZ] Only "a few select brands each year"; the team becomes "a temporary extension of the brand's own team"; extends with a global specialist network when needed. Quality over volume. — practice
- [P] Values "the slow part of the process, including the research, the sketching, and the deep thinking" as much as final high-fidelity; "never being satisfied with good enough". — opinion
- [VIS-I][VIS-L][EMO] Ottografie v2 (photography portfolio; v1 was their first Awwwards Site of the Day and later CSSDA Photography Site of the Year): instead of "familiar photography grids" they built "something that felt more like an environment than a portfolio". The interface reacts to the user in real time and serves at times as navigation, zoom tool or hint, kept minimal so "the focus stays where it belongs, on the photography". They put the photographer himself into the story with colorful silhouettes. — practice
- [EMO][VIS-M] Amaterasu (quantum computing and mental health): avoided the "cold, clinical" healthcare look; layered fluid organic visuals with an audio landscape and real personal voice stories so complexity is felt, not explained. Claimed Silver Lovie and Site of the Day on Awwwards, FWA and GSAP Showcase. — evidence (self-reported)
- [VIS-L][Q] Pixelflakes (architectural visualization studio, escaping the "portfolio trap" where everything looks like the competition): "Sketch Studies" as a draggable full-screen canvas showing the raw concept phase before the final render; heavy content moved into side panels so the UI "never competes with the renders"; pixel-inspired details kept subtle; built on a headless framework so it feels "instant, almost like using an app". — practice

## Techniques and steps
1. For a photography or portfolio site, drop the standard grid; make the interface an environment whose controls change role (navigate, zoom, hint) and stay minimal.
2. Show the process (sketches, behind the scenes) next to final images to give the viewer the "why" behind the work.
3. Move secondary content to side panels so the main gallery is never crowded.
4. Put the person behind the work into the story (silhouettes, voices) for intimacy.

## For 1000 Islands
Most relevant digest in this batch. Takeaways: (1) a photo-led site should feel like moving through a place, not like a grid of thumbnails; minimal controls so the photography stays the subject; (2) move secondary info (itinerary details, prices, FAQs) into side panels or drawers so the main imagery stays clean; (3) show the people and the process behind the trips (guides, boat, local life) for intimacy; (4) "human touch as luxury" matches an upper-middle audience, not a luxury-maximalist one. Full-screen draggable canvases and audio layers are beyond offer-minimum scope and need mobile-performance checks; copy the idea, not the build.
