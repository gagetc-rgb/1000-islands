## Core thesis
Resn's stated mission, unchanged for 20+ years, is to make the internet "a bit less shit": turn dry or complicated material into experiences people actually want to explore, with surprise and delight, and low patience for mediocrity and "same-sy" work.

## Claims and advice
- [EMO][P] Make dry material explorable: Squarespace Foundations turns brand guidelines (logo, typography, colour, motion, photography, campaign) into chapters with custom interactions and playful motion. — type: practice (self-described)
- [EMO][VIS-I] Savor (butter made from atmospheric carbon): brief was to feel luxurious, natural and delicious; they kept "a little bit science-y" but put nature at the heart, aiming for a rich, tactile, sensory site. — type: practice
- [EMO] Breakthrough Energy: focus on "what can be built" rather than what needs reducing; platform as bold as the mission, aiming to inspire optimism. — type: practice (framing choice)
- [EMO][Q] Tracing Art for Getty: turn a pile of historical records into something that feels like uncovering a historical mystery rather than data. — type: practice
- [BIZ] Recognition (FWA, Awwwards) helped them get taken seriously early, compounding over two decades. — type: opinion
- [CL][BIZ] Favourite clients share three traits: real trust, realistic budgets, and willingness to collaborate. — type: opinion
- [BIZ] Remote collaboration works for them; no single city. — type: opinion

## Techniques and steps
None described. Implied pattern: find the human story or sensation inside a dry brief (mystery, taste, optimism) and design to that.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against early-web "not really designed at all" aesthetics and against "same-sy" branded sites; jokes that awards "aren't important" until they win one.

## For 1000 Islands
Two usable ideas: the client trait list (trust, realistic budget, collaboration) fits our "scope = offer minimum" stance, and Savor's brief (sensory, tactile, delicious) is a good reference for how a photo-led site can sell a feeling of taste and place. Otherwise tone is too irreverent for an upper-middle travel audience.
