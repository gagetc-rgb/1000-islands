## Core thesis
Awards-level websites come from deliberately seeking inspiration across many sources rather than one, because a single source homogenizes your design. He offers 38 sources grouped loosely as general galleries, section-level galleries, award sites, niche sites, interaction sites, brand/portfolio sites, Pinterest-like boards and template marketplaces. His own turning point: a website of his was mocked in a feedback session, then two years later one of his sites appeared on a resource he now lists.

## Techniques and steps
1. Mix sources: at least one feed (Muzli), one section-level gallery (Unsection/Landbook), one in-motion gallery (Godly/Hover States), one outside-web board (Savee/Cosmos).
2. When a layout question arises (alignment, flow), look at the same section type across many sites and adapt, even across section types.
3. Pin or save as you go and review old saves to see how taste changes.
4. Organize saved references so they can be found later (method not given).

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Includes award sites as valid inspiration, the opposite of Jesse Showalter (see his digest) who rejects awards-based sites as ego-stroking. Includes template marketplaces, which are more typically avoided by studios.

## For 1000 Islands
Practical shortlist for a travel/hospitality reference library: Godly and Hover States (motion), Unsection/Landbook (hero, gallery and booking-CTA sections), Savee and Cosmos (photography and non-web mood), Minimal Gallery (mobile hero check), Commerce Cream (mobile-first conversion patterns, relevant for a booking CTA), Fonts In Use (type pairing in context). Using award sites for a small-scope site risks chasing effects; use them for typography and photography treatment only. The advice to save with a reason and revisit pairs with the Louis Moss tagging method.
