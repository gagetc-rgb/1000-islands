# Creative Director to CEO: How James Greenfield built Koto into a 110+ global branding studio
- Source: HOUSE OF UNKNOWN (podcast by a creative-recruitment firm; host not named in the transcript) · 2024-05-28 · 67 min · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=L9cYX2Jdo9A
- Speaker(s) and why they matter: James Greenfield, founder and CEO of Koto (branding/digital studio; Airbnb rebrand work before, then Fiverr, Diesel, Preply per the talk). A creative director who became CEO, so most advice is about agency leadership, not visual craft. Interviewer is a recruiter in the same industry (commercial interest in the creative-talent market).
- Relevance to us: 2 · Freshness matters? mostly timeless (the AI section is a 2024 view)
- Notes: captions garble names: "Cotto"/"Cot" = Koto; "hoppin" = Hopin; "Peltin" = Peloton; "Wesley Dale"/"Wale" is a joking placeholder persona. First 2 min is a teaser and a LinkedIn-profile gag. Little about design technique; two thirds is business, team and career talk.

## Core thesis
Greenfield argues that brands win through real differentiation tied to the whole customer experience (not just the logo), and that studios scale by being people-first and complementing weaknesses with a team. AI will not replace judgment about change management, but it will squeeze low-value "asset production" freelancers. For aspiring creative directors who want to be CEOs, experience and owning the whole job (including the dull parts) matter more than ambition.

## Claims and advice
- [CL][P] A designer who is not afraid of the client gets better work through: the client can "kill all of the last two weeks of work" unless engaged in what the studio is trying to achieve. (5:03-6:04) — opinion
- [BIZ] You cannot be good at everything: acknowledge weaknesses, hire or partner for them, and think "design and creativity is a team game". (7:04, 1:03:09) — opinion
- [BIZ] Companies selling creativity are best led by someone who understands the product; creative directors are rarely trusted to be CEO. (8:07-9:09) — opinion
- [CL][Q] Brand cannot make a product better than it is: a brand that promises more than the product delivers is "almost worse than having a terrible brand". Diesel case: brand, marketing and product experience were out of sync; after the rebrand dormant app accounts reactivated at above expected numbers. (14:13-17:16) — evidence (self-reported)
- [EMO][Q] Rebrand outcomes differ by client: the Preply CEO said the unexpected result was happier staff and recruiting "five times easier". (15:14) — evidence (self-reported)
- [CL] Hard conversations with skeptical founders (e.g. Fiverr CEO on a heated Zoom call) can end well; say plainly "you didn't really have a brand before". (16:15) — practice
- [Q][CL] Think about the full customer life cycle, not one part of the journey; examples used: Tesla aftercare in the UK, Peloton's "gyms are dead" assumption. (17:16-19:19) — opinion
- [CL] Example of a client whose strategy was contrary to the consultancy: Hopin (virtual events); Greenfield doubted "in-person events are dead", the project was done but never launched. (19:19-21:21) — practice
- [BIZ] Koto's growth factors: "people first" KPIs for leadership, with product as secondary internally; offices rather than isolated remote work, twice-yearly reviews, staff surveys, training (presenting, sales, mental health), an AI strategy. Greenfield says the motive is not commercial but that he does not want to spend time with the wrong people. (22:22-27:27) — practice
- [BIZ] Koto positioned itself between holding-group agencies (money in strategy, less craft) and small boutiques (craft but no access to big briefs): big briefs, small-team mentality, and the same people who pitched also deliver (no bait and switch). (11:12-13:13) — opinion
- [BIZ] Company outlook: services can only look about six months ahead; clients themselves rarely plan beyond 6 to 12 months and are internally "chaos". (29:28-30:28) — opinion
- [CL][P] Koto wants to be seen as a "collaborative partner" with strategy credentials, not only a visual service provider; a senior hire has run "300 rebrands maybe more", the way clients hire consultants for accumulated knowledge. (31:31-32:34) — opinion
- [BIZ] Agencies drift toward each other's territory (branding to digital to strategy to transformation) because clients diversify and want brand, product and marketing connected. (32:34-36:37) — opinion
- [Q] Advertising agencies are criticised (self-admitted outsider view) for still fixating on TV ads; brand work must be grounded in digital product experience. (37:38-38:39) — opinion
- [Q][EMO] Brands should live in about 10-year cycles; constant refreshes are a "sugar high"; price-comparison brands (mnemonic ads) are famous but say nothing about what they stand for. (39:40-40:40) — opinion
- [EMO][Q] Differentiator must be unique, compelling, distinctive. Airbnb example: "living like a local or belonging" vs hotel groups where art on the wall is the same in Barcelona and Berlin, a financial-cost lens that makes places feel generic. "We're living in experience economy." (40:40-42:43) — opinion
- [AI][AIS] AI will make assets, headlines and save performance-marketing cost, but "if everyone else is using exactly the same tools" there is no edge; it will not resolve internal disagreement between heads of product and marketing. Branding in five years will be "more and more about true consultancy". (42:43-43:45) — opinion, tool-dependent
- [AI][BIZ] Who loses with AI: the "below the waterline" freelancers doing day-to-day asset work (copywriters writing emails, small edits), not the visible award-winning studios. Greenfield predicts video re-editing by AI will come later. (48:50-53:58) — opinion
- [BIZ] To become a creative director/CEO you need exposure to P&L, unpaid-work disputes, new business, legal similarity claims; going too early without that fails. Founder life means doing everything including the equivalent of "putting cups in a dishwasher" once a team passes six people. (53:58-59:03) — opinion
- [BIZ] Disagrees with design directors who only mentor one person and skip reviews; leaders must own the whole cycle, "extreme ownership", or it is like wanting to be a parent "but not change nappies". (59:03-1:02:07) — opinion
- [BIZ] Career advice to his 21-year-old self: stop focusing on fairness; stop focusing on weaknesses, build a team; stop taking yourself so seriously, do dumb things so the lessons stick; accept that not everyone will like you. (1:02:07-1:06:12) — opinion
- [MB] Branding agencies are bad at branding themselves because without a client deadline they chase a "monument to the self" and stall. (47:49-48:50) — opinion

## Techniques and steps
No explicit how-to sequences. Implicit process points: engage the client in what the studio is trying to achieve before presenting; place the brand against competitors' perception; check brand, marketing and product consistency; write a clear differentiator before promising results.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Rejects the common startup advice to delegate everything except your strength: you must "own it" and do reviews. Doubts the post-pandemic idea that virtual events replace in-person ones. Sceptical that AI is a "silver bullet" for brand work. Self-aware that his critique of ad agencies is an outsider's.

## For 1000 Islands
Mostly background. Usable points: (1) differentiation must come from something real in the place and offer, not from a generic hotel/travel look (his Airbnb "belonging" and "same art in Barcelona and Berlin" examples map to a Thousand Islands site that should feel specifically local rather than generic resort); (2) do not let brand promise exceed the experience (photos and copy should match what guests actually get); (3) a clear differentiator written down before design.

## Other
