## Core thesis
Sources of taste are mostly physical and historical (books, exhibitions, drawings, prints), and the habit that matters is looking at original material and treating making, whether sketching or coding, as a way of thinking. For tools and hype waves (currently AI), be "a sailor": use the wind to go your own direction.

## Claims and advice
- [REF] The right book "is like opening a door or a window into a new world"; his formative reference was John Maeda's "Design by Numbers" (code beside images; writing about what coding feels like) and Maeda's MIT work. (0:00-2:00) — opinion
- [REF][P] Lance Wyman's "Visual Diaries" (1968 Mexico City Olympics logo, subway icons) shows that everything starts as a napkin sketch with the hand. (1:00) — opinion
- [REF] A book can carry more resolution and richness than compressed online video: he loves a documentation book of abstract film artists because YouTube versions are heavily compressed; "important to look at the original material". (4:04-5:05) — practice
- [VIS-M] "All you need to animate anything is two frames": Masahiko Sato's "Midnight Animation" (red/green print read with alternating red-green light) shows constraint-led animation. (4:04) — opinion/evidence
- [REF] "Knowing that a tool exists can change your mind": he keeps a book of tools ("Cool Tools") of which 99% is not needed, but knowing it exists matters. (6:05-7:07) — opinion
- [REF][VIS-L] Books whose layout responds to their content (Lupton and Miller "Design Writing Research", pictographs embedded in the text; each essay designed differently) show how a document itself can be interesting. (7:07-8:09) — opinion
- [EMO] Seeing a physical exhibition (clothes lifted by motors in a Paris gallery, 1990s) had profound impact; physical experience beats screens for influence. (2:00-3:02) — evidence (anecdote)
- [AI][AIS] "Waves of hype" come every 5-10 years; AI is the current one and deserves engagement, but students should be sailors: use the tools and today's voice to find their own direction. (9:10) — opinion
- [P] Drawing, coding and printmaking are all "forms of thinking"; holding a print means you can see the result differently from on-screen. (5:05, 8:09) — opinion
