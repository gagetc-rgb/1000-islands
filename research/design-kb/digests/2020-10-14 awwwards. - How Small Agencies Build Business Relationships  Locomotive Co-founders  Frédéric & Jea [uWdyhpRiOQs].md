## Core thesis
A small agency should choose clients and projects by "fit" (creative, human, business), say no to bad fits, involve the client in work sessions, nurture relationships over time and decide with both emotion and logic. In a future of automation, the value of an agency is creative and human advice.

## Claims and advice
- [CL][BIZ] Fit has three elements: creative fit (vision matches the studio style), human fit (how the client treats the relationship and the team, "protect your team from crazy clients"), business fit (money, visibility, or a great cause). (4:19-5:19) — practice
- [CL][BIZ] Learn to say no: saying yes to a misfit means missing the clients that fit; it takes blind trust that good projects will come ("I believe in karma"). (5:19-6:19) — opinion
- [CL][BIZ] Decide with emotion and logic: they say a "no" reaches the same answer from an emotional or mathematical view since time and energy are limited; they criticize agencies "into big data" for forgetting emotional decision making. (6:19-8:20) — opinion
- [CL][P] Communicate better: call the client, show your face, prioritize direct human contact. (9:21) — practice
- [CL][P] Collaborate: start with work sessions with the client; "we have half of the answer and the client owns the other half"; stay humble because no agency does great work alone. (9:21-10:22) — opinion
- [P][CL] Strategic workshops with the client look like extra investment but save time long-term, identify problems before they arise, and fix purpose, "the why" and story line for each piece to manage expectations. (10:22) — practice
- [CL][BIZ] Nurture the relationship beyond a single contact: build relationships with several people at the client since they may move companies; say "bravo" to clients, colleagues, even the barista. (10:22-11:22) — opinion
- [CL][Q] Know where to focus on budget: the client always wants more; use intuition to find what the client aims at and put effort where the impact is greatest. (11:22) — practice
- [BIZ] All three partners are involved in every project daily. (12:24) — practice
- [P][TOOLS] Define a graphic or creative style true to yourself; build a design process that suits your team; aim to beat the last best work; develop your own toolbox and use available resources rather than starting from scratch (Google developer tools; last year they experimented with text-to-speech, AR, machine learning). (12:24-13:25) — practice
- [REF][TOOLS] Share what you learn (GitHub, blogs, conferences); they open-sourced Locomotive Scroll (lerp effects, parallax, moving objects) and experimented with variable fonts for an editorial project. (13:25-14:27) — practice
- [CL][BIZ] Long-term vs serial dating: agencies often deliver a good project and move on; they pose the question whether to be a "player" (always new clients) or "hopeless romantic" (invest in existing best clients) and run a poll (result not given). Advice to keep clients: stay relevant, challenge the client, bring fresh ideas, treat him like a new prospect, check in, take responsibility for mistakes. (14:27-17:31) — opinion
- [CL] Do not default to blaming the client; introspect first. (17:31) — opinion
- [AIS][BIZ] Future: in an increasingly automated, AI-driven industry the value of a small agency is its "creative and human side"; professional human advice over automation, using automation to help humans work more efficiently. (17:31-18:31) — opinion

## Techniques and steps
1. Screen each prospect on creative, human and business fit; say no if it does not resonate.
2. Run a strategic workshop with the client before the work.
3. Call rather than email; involve the client; build contacts beyond one person.
4. Spend effort where the client's aim and the impact meet; contain the rest.
5. Keep partners in every project; share tools publicly.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against purely data-driven agency decisions; against the habit of blaming the client; against taking every project. Echoes Bierut's talk on choosing clients (see digest 23mlH8Ioycw) but with a small-agency angle.
