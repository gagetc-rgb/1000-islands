## Core thesis
A mood board is a hand-off of the general look and feel (color, pattern, texture, type, imagery) that saves days of hi-fi mockup work and helps find the right solution before real design starts. Four formats exist: strict, messy, pin board, and style tile; he prefers the style tile as the step just before mockup.

## Claims and advice
- [MB][P] Mood board = general aesthetic for a site/project handed to a client or creative director to communicate look and feel; contents include color, pattern, texture, typography, image selection. (0:00) — practice
- [MB][P] Time saving: at a former agency he had to make 3-4 full high-res mockups per website; replacing them with 3-4 quick mood boards cut preparation from days to hours. (0:00-1:00) — practice/anecdote
- [MB] Second benefit: assembling palette, textures, type and imagery shows a "snapshot" of the idea; paired with a wireframe it shows the skeleton "with a little bit of meat on the bones". (1:00) — opinion
- [MB] Third benefit: helps make sense of the brand, client and user. (1:00) — opinion, vague
- [MB] Six elements: color palette, typography, textures, patterns, photo selection, plus context-specific items (button styles for an interface, menu styles for a website). (1:00-2:01) — practice
- [MB] Format 1, strict mood board: tightly categorized and laid out, presentational; for a stricter art director or a client wanting a professional presentation; still much less work than hi-fi mockups. (2:01) — practice
- [MB] Format 2, messy mood board: screenshots and images pulled from anywhere onto one canvas "scrapbook"; he likes it, saves time, gets work done. (2:01-3:01) — practice
- [MB][TOOLS] Format 3, pin board: digital messy board; Pinterest named, plus InVision boards where images can be resized; he uses image size as a hierarchy (biggest = liked best, smallest = to be considered). (3:01) — practice
- [MB][P] Format 4, style tile: blend of strict and messy; minimal page layout (header, backdrop) that would never ship as-is; the step "before mockup but not quite as messy or abstract as a pure mood board". Found at the Style Tiles site; he rediscovered it after forgetting about it. (3:01-5:03) — practice
- [P] Mood board work comes before and replaces hi-fi mockup in the presentation step. (1:00) — practice

## Techniques and steps
1. Collect color, type, textures, patterns, photos and context elements (buttons, menus).
2. Pick a format: strict (formal clients), messy (fast), pin board (resizable hierarchy), style tile (hybrid).
3. Make 3-4 boards instead of 3-4 full mockups; present them with a wireframe if available.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Mildly against hi-fi mockups as the first presentation artifact. Otherwise conventional; no depth on how to judge or critique a board.

## For 1000 Islands
Confirms that a mood board / style tile can replace 3-4 hi-fi mockups at Stage 2 and fits our existing grey wireframes (board + wireframe = skeleton plus flesh). Style tile is the most relevant format for us: our wireframes already supply the layout, so a style tile only needs color, type, photo treatment and a few UI elements (button, menu). Use image size as a priority signal on the board. Note it is thin on method; use the Louis Moss and Flux Academy digests for annotation and presenting.
