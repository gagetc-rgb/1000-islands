## Core thesis
Mobile is not a lesser canvas: strong desktop sites translate well when the layout reduces to one or two columns, keeps a clear big/medium/small hierarchy, keeps the brand world (type, motion, details), and adapts crops, menus and interactions to touch. Each example is a quick review of what carries over from desktop.

## Claims and advice
- [MOB][Q] Mobile share of global traffic is "more like 60%", USA 47% (not 99% as commenters claim); mobile still deserves real design effort. (0:00) — evidence (unsourced)
- [MOB][VIS-L] Centered single-column layout with one big headline, a byline and one button; use an illustration whose shape (a cone/triangle) leads the eye up toward the headline. (0:00) — practice
- [MOB][VIS-L] Card grids collapse from four columns on desktop to two or often one on mobile; keep interest through playful details (cards at slight angles like stacked physical objects), scroll animations and tidy expand/collapse via plus icons. (1:00) — practice
- [MOB][VIS-L] "Big, medium, small" hierarchy: largest item (e.g. portrait) first, headline second, smaller elements third. (2:01, 6:10) — practice
- [MOB][EMO] Small touches carry brand world on mobile: scrolling lines, faint background grid, animated 3D shapes, per-letter text animation, angled corners. Visitors should feel they "participate" in a brand world. (2:01-3:04) — opinion
- [MOB][VIS-I] Choose aspect ratios for the phone: a model-portfolio site uses portrait crops (full-length human shots suit the vertical format) so scrolling feels social. (3:04-4:04) — practice
- [MOB][VIS-L] Generous white space is possible on mobile and gives a "premium, calming" effect; two columns then one, sensible point size readable on a phone without overwhelming. (4:04-5:07) — practice
- [MOB][VIS-M] No hover or cursor-following on touch, but other interaction layers exist: touch-and-drag, horizontal scrolling lists, layered scroll animations (e.g. a car skeleton growing as the next layer slides over), page transitions. (5:07-6:10) — practice
- [MOB][VIS-I] Full-screen video heroes can work on mobile; one example overlays semi-transparent cards at the bottom; another works because the wide-screen footage was shot so the narrow crop still carries the story. (6:10-7:12, 9:15) — practice
- [MOB] Menu patterns: a hamburger that opens a near-full-screen overlay is common and tidy; place the menu button at the bottom near the thumb (dance site example). Notes legibility over video can be poor. (7:12, 9:15) — practice
- [MOB][VIS-T] Clean mobile type: good contrast, good point size, left-aligned ragged right, simple headline + body + image, with the odd animation not overwhelming; typography plus a consistent brand look is "really enough". (7:12-8:14) — opinion
- [MOB][Q] Put the work up front instead of burying it under clicks (architect site: stacked image loading animation, draggable project strip, then click in); for decision-heavy purchases people may switch to desktop, but mobile should give the flavor. (8:14-9:15) — opinion
- [VIS-L][EMO] Make each panel poster-worthy: "each one individually could almost be a poster" you could print and hang on the studio wall. Attributed to advice he received. (10:16) — opinion
- [VIS-I][EMO] Present products like sculptural objects (floating, exploded parts) to make them "objects of desire"; an Apple-like scroll feel can be recreated on mobile while diverging in type and layout. (11:18-12:20) — opinion
- [MOB][VIS-L] Asymmetric, windowed and layered layouts still work on mobile: background image fades in opacity during scroll to make type legible; text can sit over photographs; section borders can be stepped. (12:20-13:20) — practice
- [Q][MOB] Closing: "no excuse" for not making designs accessible on mobile, no reason desktop excellence cannot translate. (13:20) — opinion

## Techniques and steps
1. Decide hierarchy first: big, medium, small.
2. Collapse grids to 2 then 1 column; replace hover states with tap/drag/scroll interactions.
3. Re-crop imagery for portrait (shoot or select with the narrow crop in mind).
4. Full-screen menu overlay, button within thumb reach.
5. Keep brand details (lines, grid, per-letter text animation) so mobile feels like the same world.
6. Check each screen as if it were a poster.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against the claim that mobile has less scope for design flair and against the "99% of users are on phones" comment trope (cites 60% / 47%). Mildly against low-contrast menus over video, though he tolerates them.

## For 1000 Islands
Directly relevant for a photo-led site: plan portrait crops for hero and gallery images (a vertical framing of islands, boats, people) rather than just cropping the desktop shot; photograph with a safe center area. Use one-column cards, a bottom thumb-reach menu, bottom sticky CTA in line with our mobile sticky bar rules, left-aligned text over a darkened or faded photo for legibility (fade the image opacity as text scrolls). Full-screen video hero is acceptable if footage is framed for both ratios. "Poster test" per panel is a quick review gate. Skip heavy 3D and scroll-jacking: not in the offer minimum.

## Other
Remark that a portfolio-type site is better with work shown up front (relevant to a gallery-first homepage).
