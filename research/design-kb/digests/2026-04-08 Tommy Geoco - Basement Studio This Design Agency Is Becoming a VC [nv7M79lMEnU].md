# Basement Studio: This Design Agency Is Becoming a VC
- Source: Tommy Geoco (YouTube, "State of Play" interview series) · 2026-04-08 · 35 min · https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nv7M79lMEnU
- Speaker(s) and why they matter: Tommy Geoco (host, runs his own small media studio and is scaling it); Basement Studio founders Facundo and Jose (names as spoken; the other speaker names in captions, e.g. "Hugo", "Paco", "Facu", are inconsistent, verify). Basement is a ~35-person studio from Argentina doing WebGL-heavy sites for Vercel, Kidsuper, MrBeast. They promote their own open-source tools (XMCP, a headless CMS), so there is a self-promotion angle.

## Core thesis
Basement runs three divisions (client services, an R&D lab, a microfund "Basement Ventures") so that lab experiments feed client work and sometimes become products. Quality is protected by internal pride tests, whole-company review before launch, and keeping a lab where the team can take big swings. Useful as a studio-model case study, thin as a design how-to.

## Claims and advice
- [BIZ] Three divisions: client services, an in-house lab, and Basement Ventures (microfund incubating startups); the lab discovers opportunities that become ventures (XMCP, a headless CMS spun off as its own company). (0:00-15:17) — evidence (self-reported)
- [BIZ][EMO] Kidsuper World started as a lab proof of concept: a small site selling one famous mini bag, built by a team of one art director, one 3D artist, one illustrator, one UI designer and two 3D developers; the client liked it and it grew into a larger project. Contact started at Next.js Conf. (8:08-10:11) — evidence
- [P][VIS-I] The Kidsuper look was developed in a Figma file by drawing illustrations and using photos from the studio; hand-painted illustrations and an actual painting were made by in-house artists, then turned into WebGL experiences. (9:10) — evidence
- [VIS-I] They poured real wine on a surface, scanned it and used it as a texture for a WebGL shader ("the best way just to recreate that texture"). (0:00, 26:31) — evidence/anecdote
- [Q] Internal quality test: "are you proud of it, will you show it to your friends or your mom", plus metrics and public reaction; admits a lot of gut feeling and subjectivity. At every release they ask whether they are proud and how to raise the bar next time. (5:05-7:07) — practice
- [Q][P] A Slack "review" channel: before launch the whole company (about 30 of 35 people) hunts for small tweaks, no hard feelings; they call that last 1% the hardest and most important part. (20:25-21:25) — practice
- [P] Process change: previously idea, then design, then development; now developers start from day zero on a website and use that time to explore experiences, some of which are not used on the project and go into lab work. (21:25-22:25) — practice
- [BIZ] Art director hiring profile (they could not fill the role): guides designers, loves building, can articulate feedback and "sell directions" to clients, and understands code well enough to know how Figma designs become interactive. Art directors work in the Figma file with designers and help them present. (22:25-25:30) — opinion/practice
- [AI][BIZ] AI is "a tsunami" to learn to surf: they build a site every 3-4 weeks and tried an MCP-fed scaffolder for websites from their own components (nav, CTA); building an MCP took 3-5 days, so they made XMCP (a framework to configure MCP apps with one command). (11:12-13:15) — evidence (tool-dependent)
- [AI] The entry bar to making things dropped with AI, so the bar for quality goes up; projects that took two months now take one. Do not forget "nostalgia" and the old tools that still work. (26:31-28:33) — opinion
- [BIZ] Open-sourcing is a way to give back to the community and invite pull requests; they also worked on the Geist font for Vercel, now free on Google Fonts. (29:35-31:36) — evidence
- [BIZ] Margins from projects were reinvested into the lab and ventures; some projects were done for no money to validate a use case; need cash reserves to afford that. (18:21) — practice
- [BIZ] Lab ideas: many ideas jammed, those that survive are sketched quickly and validated rapidly. (19:23-20:25) — practice
- [BIZ] Host's closing view: most agencies optimise for efficiency and get "safe"; Basement poured margins into the lab as a compounding loop. (33:38) — opinion

## Techniques and steps
1. Prove a client idea with a small proof of concept built by a mixed micro-team (AD, 3D, illustrator, UI, two developers), then expand if the client bites.
2. Before launch, put the live link in a company-wide review channel and collect small fixes from everyone.
3. Start development from day zero in parallel with design to explore interaction.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against the usual agency path of trying a side SaaS and failing: they say the lab and venture arms were built in from the start and funded by margins. Against letting AI replace older, still-valid tools.

## For 1000 Islands
Little direct use. Takeaways: a "does it make us proud, would we show it to our mom" test and a one-pass review of the live link by several fresh pairs of eyes before showing the owner; a small proof of concept (one hero section) before building the whole site.
