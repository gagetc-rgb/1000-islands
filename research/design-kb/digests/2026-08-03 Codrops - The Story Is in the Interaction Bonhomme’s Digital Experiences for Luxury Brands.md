## Core thesis
Brand experience lives in the interaction: precision in every interaction, clarity in narrative, and alignment between what a brand wants to express and what users experience. Technology should "serve the story", and a small founder-led team keeps continuity from vision to final validation.

## Claims and advice
- [P][BIZ] Deliberately compact, founder-led: the two founders are personally involved in every project from start to end; the people who define the vision validate the result. — type: opinion
- [P] UX starts with a Design Sprint-based discovery: stakeholder interviews, user journeys, empathy mapping, collaborative workshops before any interface is designed. — type: practice
- [P][TEST] User flows are prototyped, tested and refined through successive iterations; the interface is "a response to real user behaviors", not a visual proposal. — type: practice
- [Q][CL] Premium e-commerce should extend the brand experience, not interrupt it; purchase as continuation of the journey. Starts with a customer experience audit (user research, qualitative interviews, journey analysis) to balance artistic direction and conversion. — type: practice
- [P][Q] Corporate sites: design the information first. Analyse communication objectives, audiences and content ecosystem, define information architecture, then structure navigation around user journeys. — type: practice
- [DS] Documented design system of reusable components for long-term consistency; delivered as fully documented Figma libraries covering visual language, interaction principles, typography, grids, colour palettes, interface states. Built for the teams who maintain the product after launch. — type: practice
- [VIS-M][Q] WebGL used "not as visual effects for their own sake, but as storytelling tools"; every experience engineered for browser compatibility, loading speed and rendering optimisation so immersion does not cost usability. Examples: TAG Heuer Carrera Skipper (real-time 3D ocean), Lacoste Heritage, Audi Dare TT, Bonobo. — type: practice
- [MOB] Mobile apps start with user research, personas, journeys, information architecture; modular interfaces that adapt across devices and screen sizes (Festival de Cannes app, Audi Spotted). — type: practice
- [EMO] Installations (Cartier on Rue de la Paix, Coca-Cola, Renault, Dacia) use the same disciplines, with reliability and ease of use critical in physical space. — type: practice
- [EMO] "Technology should always serve the story, not the other way around." — type: opinion

## Techniques and steps
1. Structured discovery: stakeholder interviews, user journeys, empathy mapping, workshops.
2. Customer experience audit (for e-commerce): research, interviews, journey analysis.
3. Define information architecture from communication goals and audiences.
4. Prototype flows, test, iterate.
5. Build on a documented design system (Figma library) for later teams.
6. Engineer WebGL for compatibility and speed.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against treating commerce as a "transactional break" from the brand; against design and development as separate disciplines. No explicit attack on other studios.

## For 1000 Islands
Relevant structure: a short discovery with the owner before design (interviews, journeys of a visitor from first photo to booking enquiry); information architecture before visuals; purchase or enquiry that feels like a continuation of the journey, not a form dumped at the end. Premium feeling comes from precision and clarity, not effects; keep WebGL out, and test speed on mobile. Design system in Figma is not needed at this scope, but named colour/type tokens in HTML are.
