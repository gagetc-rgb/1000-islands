# How Art Directors ACTUALLY Build Moodboards

## Core thesis
A mood board is not a collection of things you like; it is a creative argument where every image is a sentence and must have a stated reason to be there. The skill is analytical referencing: knowing exactly what you take from each reference, not collecting by attraction.

## Claims and advice
- [MB] Standard: "can I feel the project in under 10 seconds?" If not, change the board. A good board shows the whole finished dish (tone, aesthetic, approach), not the ingredients. (1:02-2:04) — practice
- [MB] Two different documents: an internal reference board (loose, exploratory, near-duplicates allowed) and a client-facing direction board (few images, considered, harmonious). (2:04) — practice
- [MB] Even the internal board: every reference must earn its place. Name what you take from it (color, composition, texture, cultural reference); if you cannot, delete it. (2:04-3:05) — practice
- [MB] Volume trap: 50 references and you forget what each one is for. More references = less clarity; fewer, genuinely great ones win. (3:05) — opinion from experience
- [MB] Client board: images must sit together as one object, not just look good individually. A strong reference that looks jarring should be recolored or edited in Photoshop to fit. If it stands out to you, it will stand out to the client and be misread. (5:06) — practice
- [MB] No weak assets on a client board (pixelated, badly cropped): it undermines the idea before you speak. (5:06) — practice
- [MB] Image size is an editorial decision: bigger images get more psychological weight, so the references most central to the direction get the most space. (5:06-6:08) — practice
- [MB][CL] Add short captions on client boards (or on a following page) saying what exactly is referenced: palette, photo treatment, material, typographic mood. Reason: a client may react negatively to an element that is not even part of what you are referencing, and once you are on the back foot the idea usually dies. Be top-line and direct, not over-explaining. (6:08-7:09) — practice, from repeated bad experience
- [MB][CL] An art director's direction has financial implications (premium materials, photographers, locations, effects). The board must make the "why" visible so it reads as considered and intentional. (7:09-8:09) — practice
- [MB] Art director board vs designer board: the AD board is holistic (photography direction, color territories, type moods, materials, tone, graphics as one part); the designer board zooms into one element (type system, graphic language, composition) as evidence for the AD's argument. Mixing them makes the project lose clarity and people pull in different directions. (8:09-10:11) — practice
- [MB] The moodboard is the foundation: if it is unclear, everything after it is unclear. (10:11) — opinion
- [REF] Most people reference by attraction ("collecting"); real referencing asks why: color combination, composition, asset quality (textures, fabrics), cultural reference, lighting, mood. (10:11-11:11) — practice
- [REF] Combine several specific qualities from different references into something ownable; "that's not copying, that's how creativity has always worked". Nothing is original, combinations can be. (11:11) — opinion
- [REF] Categorize references as you save them with the reason (color? image treatment?). Doing this consistently teaches you your own taste and its patterns, which feeds your choices. (11:11-12:12) — practice
- [REF][TOOLS] Sources: Pinterest and Instagram are fine but algorithm-fed (everyone gets the same references; Pinterest is also full of ads), so output can converge. Are.na is preferred: boards built by intentional curators, higher standard, less but better. Use multiple sources. Quality of references shapes quality of output. (12:12-13:14) — opinion
- [Q][EMO] Closing claim: when the board reflects clarity, the audience "doesn't always know why, but they feel it". (14:14) — opinion

## Techniques and steps
1. Build an internal board freely; for each image write what you take from it; delete anything you cannot justify.
2. For the client board: select few images; edit them to harmonize; size by importance; add one-line captions naming what is referenced; make the reason for costly choices visible.
3. Test: show it to someone, can they get the project in 10 seconds?
4. When saving references, tag by quality (color / treatment / composition / material / cultural).
