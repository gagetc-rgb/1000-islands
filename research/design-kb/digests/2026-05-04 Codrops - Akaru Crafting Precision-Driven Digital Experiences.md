## Core thesis
Akaru claims design and performance are inseparable ("Designed to perform. Coded to seduce."): bespoke platforms, no templates, with a four-phase method (UX sessions, production, performance, long-term support) and technology used "only when it enhances the experience". Their pitch, "Creative Commerce", is to turn e-commerce into a brand experience without losing conversion.

## Techniques and steps
1. Begin with collaborative UX workshops to fix strategy and information architecture before design.
2. Build a design system in production alongside development.
3. Treat SEO and performance optimization as its own phase, not an afterthought.
4. Plan ongoing support and improvement after launch.
5. For e-commerce or conversion pages, keep one emotional hero (immersive visuals) but keep the path to purchase structured and fast.

## For 1000 Islands
Moderately useful: (1) the four-phase order (workshop for structure, build, performance pass, support) mirrors our staged pipeline and gives a defensible list of what the client gets; (2) "balancing emotion and conversion" (Vaonis) is the exact tension of a photo-led travel site with an inquiry or booking CTA: keep immersive imagery but a structured path to the enquiry; (3) real-estate "each space becomes a narrative" suggests presenting each island or tour as a story, not a listing; (4) their rule to use tech only when it helps matches our offer-minimum, lightweight HTML approach. SEO/SEA, content production and long-term support are extras beyond our offer minimum (paid).
