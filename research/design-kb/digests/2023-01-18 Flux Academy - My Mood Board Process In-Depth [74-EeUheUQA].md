- Speaker and why they matter: unnamed brand designer from South Africa (name not stated in transcript; Flux Academy instructor, verify). Runs strategy plus brand identity for clients; Flux Academy sells design courses and a student community (light commercial interest; mentions Loom use with students).
- Relevance to us: 5 · Freshness: timeless (process); Pinterest/Illustrator/Loom are incidental
- Notes: no sponsor segment. Worked example is a brand-identity project (boutique hotel), so logo/stationery parts transfer less to a web project. Screen-recording narration; long stretches (14:16-28:29) are Illustrator mechanics, low value except as a method of balancing a board.

## Core thesis
After a rigorous discovery and strategy phase, present the client two moodboard concepts (rarely three): one "expected/safer" and one with more creative finesse, aimed at an aspirational audience or the other end of a personality tension. Annotate each board, present it live or by video, and finish with a side-by-side comparison; the client then signs off a final board before design begins.

## Claims and advice
- [MB][CL] Always present two concepts, very rarely three: after good discovery there are few genuinely different directions that serve the audience and goals. (0:00) — practice
- [MB] Direction 1 = expected, safer, comfortable for the client, fits industry; Direction 2 = more creative finesse, off the cuff, or inspired by the aspirational audience (e.g. a new client base the client wants to reach). (0:00-1:01) — practice
- [MB] Other ways to split directions: by audience, by two goals, or by the two ends of a personality disparity heard in discovery (show both ends of the scale). (1:01) — practice
- [MB][P] Start with a brain dump / word map / mind map in a notebook before browsing, so you have "goggles on": otherwise you can lose days scrolling Pinterest. (2:03) — practice
- [MB] Example brief: boutique luxury hotel with three destinations in South Africa (beach, bushveld/safari, mountain); client wants to keep boutique, classic, traditional feel but attract a slightly younger (older-millennial and beyond) audience, couples, small families, corporate events; competing against Airbnb and big hotel names. Mind map words: classic, eclectic, something unexpected, warm traditional tones, heritage, family. (3:05-5:08) — evidence/example
- [MB] A board must contain things that help future decisions: logo-style groups (serif vs sans, ornate vs simple), color palette (noted on the board so the client aligns), stationery/real-world applications, illustration/graphic style/pattern, lived typography (not just logo type), optional photographic/art direction. (5:08-8:10) — practice
- [MB][CL] Do not include references irrelevant to the project (e.g. business cards if client says none); it must be custom, not a "cookie cutter mood board straight off of Pinterest". (8:10-9:11) — practice
- [REF][TOOLS] Pinterest trick: clicking a pin and scrolling down shows similar references, "really helpful". (17:21) — practice
- [MB] Cull: collect broadly in Pinterest, then at the end delete what no longer fits (example: too rustic for the destinations); the raw board can stand alone but is not what you show the client; show a curated version. (13:15-14:16) — practice
- [MB][VIS-L] Layout: 1080x1080 artboard with nine squares; group references by type (stationery, texture/embossing, illustration, logo style, photography); if a category is thin (only one logo reference) go back and gather more so the board covers everything. (14:16-20:24) — practice
- [MB][VIS-L] Balance: lighter pieces in the middle squares, darker in the corners; alternate photographic with digital pieces so the eye can "dance around"; small images can be shrunk and the frame colour-picked to blend. (22:27-23:28) — practice
- [MB][VIS-C] Centre of the board holds either the colour palette (swatches pulled with colour picker, two dark colours not adjacent) or a reference image of the target audience pulled from the strategy personas. Add disclaimer that the palette may shift slightly. (24:28-28:29) — practice
- [MB] Concepts may overlap when the brand effectively has one direction (hotel example: logo almost the same in both, differences in colour, illustration and texture); overlap shows a single-minded brand. (29:32-32:40) — opinion
- [MB][VIS-I] In a real hospitality project use photographs of the actual lodges/rooms, to test whether the branding supports what the place looks like. (30:36) — practice
- [CL][P] Presentation matters "almost more important if not the most important part of the designer process": clients often have never seen a mood board, so guide them through why the step matters and how to give feedback. (33:42-34:43) — opinion
- [CL] Presentation deck template ("creative direction template"): who it is for, prepared by, contact; reminder of why this step matters; recap of strategy, brand values, primary goal; then each concept with a name (e.g. "making a statement", "the refined professional", "boldly unique"). (33:42-36:46) — practice
- [MB][CL] Label images on the board so the client can give feedback on "image 4" instead of "third from the left"; descriptions reference one or two images. (35:45) — practice
- [MB][CL] Per concept, speak to: core concept, colour, type choices, imagery choices, then texture/pattern/creative devices (illustration, shapes); always include a palette. (35:45) — practice
- [MB][CL] Third "wild card" concept (rare): used when the client talked about wanting to stand out; she was unsure he would like it; he chose it, and the final blended it with the other concept (more structured line work, orange toned down, green added). (37:46-40:51) — evidence (one anecdote)
- [CL] Force clarity: the contrast between directions makes the client state key priorities (refined/clean vs edge/grunge) so the identity does not try to be everything. (37:46) — practice
- [CL] Second-to-last slide is a side-by-side comparison of all concepts so the client can decide. (40:51) — practice
- [CL][TOOLS] If shy or nervous presenting live, record a Loom video walkthrough; clients may not read captions, so voice and tone add information; still recommends some conversation about direction. (40:51-42:55) — practice
- [P][CL] After feedback and iteration, a final mood board is signed off by the client before the design phase; it is then included in the style guide at the end. (42:55) — practice

## Techniques and steps
1. Discovery and strategy first; derive the audience and personality tension.
2. Mind map words on paper.
3. Collect in a Pinterest board; cull.
4. Define two directions (expected vs creative; or two audiences/ends of a scale); optional third wild card.
5. Build each board: group by category, balance light/dark, palette or audience persona in the centre, labelled images.
6. Name each concept; export JPEGs; put into the creative-direction deck: strategy recap, concept core idea, colour, type, imagery, texture, palette; comparison slide.
7. Present live or via Loom; collect feedback; blend if needed; client signs off the board; move to design.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against showing three very different directions (common agency practice): two is usually enough after strategy. Against the raw Pinterest dump as a deliverable. Treats overlap between directions as a feature, not a failure.

## For 1000 Islands
Most directly applicable method: two directions for the owner, one expected/safe travel look and one aspirational "wild card" (maybe aimed at the upper-middle audience the owner wants to attract), each named, labelled per image, with a palette in the middle and a comparison slide; walk the owner through it by Loom video since we do not meet live, and get a written sign-off of the final board (fits our PIPELINE sign-offs). Use real photographs of the islands on the board so the direction is tested against actual material. Skip logo/stationery parts (out of offer-minimum scope).

## Other
Template detail: deck always includes "who prepared" and contact. She reuses the signed-off board in the style guide at project end.
