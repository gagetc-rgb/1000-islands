## Core thesis
A studio does better work when the team environment ("play") and the working environment ("work": process and code) are both designed around people, with empathy as the shared principle between UX and employee experience. For process: work from abstract to concrete, prototype in code early and present work in context to the client.

## Claims and advice
- [P] Treat the process as a planet growing from its core, not a line from A to B: start with analysis and UX strategy together with content strategy, then stop to prototype and test risky ideas, then go into content, design, development, QA, deployment. The whole process is "your UX", not just the wireframe. (6:25-7:26) — practice
- [P] Work "abstract to concrete" and "in context", and adapt constantly; there is "no perfect process", only good collaboration and communication. (6:25, 9:28) — opinion
- [P][AI] Prototype directly in code instead of sketching on an artboard. They cite "state of UX 2019" calling this a trend. Analogy: a sculptor jumps into the clay rather than sketching all day. (7:26) — practice (dated trend claim, tool-era 2019)
- [P] Their own number: when they started prototyping in code about 2 years earlier, about 50% of the prototype went into the final product; by 2019 about 80% of the ideas did. (8:28) — evidence (self-reported, no method given)
- [P][CL] Biggest benefit for the creative director: present the work in context to the client (show it on the phone or device). Called a "magic potion": the client can project themselves into it, understands a month of talk at once, and trust is activated. (8:28-9:28) — practice
- [P] Bring people from different roles into the same room: a UX designer into a dev meeting, a developer into a strategy meeting; also bring the client into workshops. (9:28-10:30) — practice
- [CL] "You need to adapt, not the client": the adapting is the studio's job. (10:30) — opinion
- [BIZ] Hire people more talented than you; the business-side co-founder says do not try to be creative, "limit the damage but always encourage creativity". That was the turning point of the studio's growth. (0:00-1:02) — opinion
- [BIZ] Origin story: 12 years earlier they had no money, no leads, no clients; the creative director mocked up work at night while employed elsewhere so the other partner could pitch random clients. (1:02-2:03) — evidence (anecdote)
- [BIZ] Put people first: UX and employee experience share empathy; leaders craft a "journey" for individuals just as a UX designer does for users. (4:24-5:24) — opinion
- [BIZ] Culture practices they list: hire talented people, choose "people over everything else", tolerance/flexibility/kindness, people leave the office at a decent hour, one-on-one conversations (goals, feedback, individual needs: parent, shorter week, dietary choices), "micro appreciation" like micro-interactions, celebrate each delivered project. (12:33-15:36) — practice
- [BIZ] Choose projects "with meaning" using three tests: how the team relates to it (Heart/"ride", garbled, verify), where it can lead (vision), what can be learned (knowledge); involve the team in choosing projects. (15:36) — practice
- [EMO][BIZ] Trigger an emotional response in everyone you work with, team and clients alike; "make them feel important". (15:36) — opinion
- [BIZ] Claimed outcome of people-first: more productivity, innovation, award-winning projects, brand awareness. No numbers. (16:37) — opinion, unverified

## Techniques and steps
1. Start with research and UX strategy alongside content strategy.
2. Before design/dev proper, stop and prototype and test the boldest ideas.
3. Prototype in code, so design and dev collaborate early.
4. Show the client the work on a real device, in context.
5. Run client workshops, mix roles in meetings.
