## Core thesis
The studio presents itself as a design-and-technology agency built to avoid tech "burnout culture": people first, craft second, interactive storytelling as the output. Four showcase projects (Cartier, Neko Health, Blobmixer, Pluto/Qualcomm) are offered as proof that emotional, award-winning work and a calm team culture can coexist.

## Claims and advice
- [BIZ] Founded 2011 out of frustration with "burnout culture"; aim is a place that supports curiosity, work-life balance and craftsmanship. Started as a development partner, grew into a full-service design and technology agency. — opinion (self-description)
- [BIZ] Client list given: Google, Qualcomm, the United Nations, Spotify, Cartier; multiple awards and an Awwwards Agency of the Year nomination (their claim). — evidence (unverified)
- [BIZ] Team: tight-knit, multi-national, "without heavy hierarchy", work from anywhere, hack days and internal lab projects; culture words are creativity, craftsmanship, care. — opinion
- [EMO][VIS-M] Cartier Yearbook 2024: multilingual, immersive web experience combining motion, interactivity and "elegant editorial design" to tell the brand's creative journey through 32 articles. Won Site of the Day on Awwwards, FWA and CSS Design Awards plus an Awwwards Developer Award (claimed). — evidence (self-reported)
- [EMO][VIS-I] Neko Health (preventive full-body scans): "emotional storytelling, interactive visuals, and art-directed photography" to inspire trust in new users. Claim: all clinic slots booked immediately at launch; Lovie Awards Gold 2023, FWA and CSS Design Awards Site of the Day. — evidence (self-reported; the booking claim is a conversion outcome, no figures)
- [VIS-M][Q] Blobmixer: internal passion project, WebGL toy for generating and manipulating 3D "blob" forms; "over 4 million views globally"; Awwwards, CSS Design Awards, FWA Site of the Day plus Developer Award. Shows the studio uses self-initiated experiments as marketing and as a talent magnet. — evidence (self-reported)
- [VIS-M][AI] Pluto / Qualcomm: "spatial-first" brand identity, site and developer portal that moves between 2D (mobile, desktop) and AR/VR; claimed as possibly the first spatial website; led to Qualcomm Snapdragon XR branding work. — evidence (self-reported). Tool-dependent (XR).
- [AI] Closing line mentions "helping clients transform their products and experiences in the age of AI"; no detail given. — vague
