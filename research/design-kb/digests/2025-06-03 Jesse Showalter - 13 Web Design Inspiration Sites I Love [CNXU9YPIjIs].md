- Speaker(s) and why they matter: Jesse Showalter, web designer who moved his client and agency work to Framer (stated 1:00-2:00); has a commercial interest in Framer. Practitioner with a short curated list.

## Core thesis
Good inspiration sites are fast "browsing experiences" with filtering and a one-click path to the live site. He deliberately avoids award sites because he thinks they exist to flatter designers rather than help clients, and he adds non-web sources to avoid staying inside the medium.

## Claims and advice
- [REF][Q] Avoid awards-based sites (Awwwards, FWA, CSS Design Awards) for inspiration: their purpose is "to stroke the ego of other designers", not to inspire work that serves clients or companies. (0:00) — opinion
- [REF] Look outside the medium: Savee covers architecture, furniture, 3D and logo design; "it's healthy to look outside of the medium in which you're designing". (0:00-1:00) — opinion
- [REF][VIS-T] Fonts In Use shows type in real context, more useful than static specimens on Google Fonts; he scans it for playful ways to use type. (1:00) — practice
- [REF][TOOLS] Framer Gallery: shows what is possible in Framer, with author, short description and live site; useful when the tool is the constraint. (1:00-2:00) — practice
- [REF][TOOLS] Site of Sites: lots of work, each with an about section, live link, platform and credit, plus a "best parts" breakdown; also a model for presenting your own work. (2:00-3:02) — practice
- [REF][TOOLS] Lapa Ninja: dependable but with many ads. Minimal Gallery: filter by one-page, agency, e-commerce; click goes straight to the live site. Godly: filters for websites, mobile apps, desktop apps; each entry lists technology, fonts and frameworks. Landingfolio: filter by industry, plus Figma and Tailwind component galleries. (3:02-5:04) — practice
- [REF][TOOLS] Killer Portfolio: portfolio-only sites (personal, company, design, developer) with short case-study-style notes. (5:04) — practice
- [REF][VIS-L][TOOLS] Unsection: individual sections (hero, CTA) with tags such as light, vector, minimal, software, technology. (6:05) — practice
- [REF][TOOLS] One Page Love: one-page layouts with category, features tags and left/right browsing arrows; often Webflow sites, which he does not mind. (6:05-7:05) — practice
- [REF][MOB][Q] Commerce Cream for e-commerce: shows how others write copy, lay out products and lead users; shown mobile-first because most browsing and spending happens on mobile, so think e-commerce mobile-first. (7:05-8:06) — opinion
- [REF][VIS-L] Brutalist Websites ("the punk rock of design"): off-grid, against-the-norm sites to push your limits and "break the rules, have some fun". (8:06) — opinion
- [REF] He wants "as much inspiration as I possibly can at one time", fast and efficient; filterable, one-click, tag-rich galleries fit that. (5:04, 7:05) — opinion

## Techniques and steps
1. Start with general design sources (Savee) and type-in-context (Fonts In Use) before web galleries.
2. Use tool-specific galleries (Framer Gallery) to see what is feasible.
3. Filter by industry, tag or section type (Landingfolio, Unsection, Minimal Gallery) rather than scrolling everything.
4. Click straight through to the live site to experience scroll and interaction.
5. For commerce, study mobile first.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Rejects award sites, directly opposed to the Self-Made Web Designer list that includes five of them. Biased toward Framer.

## For 1000 Islands
Use his filters: Godly and Minimal Gallery for hospitality/travel mobile heroes, Unsection for hero and CTA sections, Savee for architecture, light and photography outside web. His "look outside the medium" point supports a moodboard that mixes travel photography, boat and water details and print, not just websites. His warning about awards matters for our small scope: copy the clarity of good sites, not their effects.
