## Core thesis
AI reasons well about design but lacks the reference step: how shipped products actually handle a specific moment. Fix it by having the agent research real examples first, then design from them, then inspect the references yourself rather than trusting the AI's summary. Femke frames this as "better input", not a better prompt.

## Claims and advice
- [AI][REF] AI is already good at design reasoning (flows, sequencing, trust moments, error states) if given a solid brief, but reasoning gets you to the right answer "in theory, references get you to the right answer in practice". (0:00) — opinion
- [AIS][REF] Her diagnosis of generic AI output: the issue is not whether it can think about design, but that it "is missing that reference step entirely"; better prompting helps only up to a point because the limit is what the AI has access to. (4:04) — opinion
- [REF] Real designers never start from nothing: they pull competitors and similar moments, and a design conversation becomes stronger as "here are five examples, here is the pattern, here is why we should do it differently" versus "this is how it should work". (3:04) — opinion
- [AI][P] Order matters: first ask the agent to research only (no design yet) on specific moments, here how KYC asks are sequenced, how wait times are communicated and how trust is built before and after sensitive input; then feed findings back into the design request. (6:08-7:11) — practice
- [AI] The agent output without references was 14 screens, "not bad" but generic; with references she cut it to seven steps, and the second version added up-front expectation-setting (what is asked, roughly how long), a stepper, and reassurance (encryption, no credit-score impact) before the big ask. (2:02, 9:14-11:17) — evidence (single demo)
- [AI][Q] Make the agent cite which reference drove each decision (e.g. "show the three-phase path up front, as N26 does"; checklist sub-steps as Stake and N26), so you can verify instead of "taking Claude's word". (9:14-11:17) — practice
- [AIS][Q] "I don't want AI summarizing examples and expecting me to just trust the summary. I want to inspect the references myself." Human keeps the judgment. (11:17) — opinion
- [AIS] References do not replace design work: a pattern that suits one product (her example name garbled, verify) may be wrong for yours; you still need to know users, constraints and business model. (12:18) — opinion
- [AI] Visual board output: the agent grouped screens from N26, Wise and Chime into sections matching her questions, which she finds more useful than a text summary. (7:11-8:13) — practice (tool-dependent)

## Techniques and steps
1. Write a PRD or brief and give it to the agent in the project.
2. Ask for research first: name the specific moments and the apps in the category; ask for common patterns, grouped visually.
3. Inspect the collected references yourself.
4. Re-prompt the design with the references, adding a constraint (e.g. halve the step count), and require rationale tied to each reference.
5. Decide yourself what applies to your users.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Counters the common advice to fix weak AI output by prompting harder; says the missing piece is evidence from shipped products. Note the conflict of interest: the recommended fix is the sponsor's paid product.

## For 1000 Islands
The transferable idea: before asking the agent to design the hero or page flow, have it study real travel and hospitality sites (Stage 2 reference library) and cite which reference drives each choice; we inspect the references ourselves. Mobbin is app-screen focused, so it is of limited use for a photo-led travel site; use Are.na, Awwwards-type sites or manual screenshots instead. No need to buy it.
