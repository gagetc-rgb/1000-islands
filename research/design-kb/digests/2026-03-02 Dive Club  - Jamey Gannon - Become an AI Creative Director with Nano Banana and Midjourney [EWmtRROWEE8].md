## Core thesis
AI image work is a direction problem, not a prompt problem: models understand images and vibes better than any human can describe them, so start from a moodboard of reference images, use short prompts, iterate in small steps, and use a different model for each job. Taste comes from daily collecting and broad cultural input, not from design books.

## Techniques and steps
1. Make a mood board from references (Pinterest/Cosmos) defining camera style, vibe, model/scene.
2. In Midjourney upload as style references (--sref); try mood board codes only as a first test.
3. Judge each result against the board honestly; remove references that add unwanted colour or contrast issues.
4. Use very short prompts while testing.
5. Add a profile code (rank images you like) for personal taste.
6. Move to Nano Banana Pro for surgical fixes: one or two instructions per pass, keep position/size same, remove logos.
7. Feed good outputs back as references; build a master prompt (parameters, refs, profile codes) for the client.
8. Maintain swappable mini mood boards (real skin, product photography, logos).

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against the "prompt engineer" culture and prompt packs (she calls them "prompt bros"); against creative-director books; against agency scaling. Argues AI is a real art form requiring process, in opposition to the "anyone can do it" view.
