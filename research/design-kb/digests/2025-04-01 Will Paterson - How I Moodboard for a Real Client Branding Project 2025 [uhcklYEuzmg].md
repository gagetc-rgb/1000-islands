## Core thesis
A mood board aligns client and designer on visual direction before any logo is drawn, which makes final approval faster. Build it by mind-mapping brand keywords first, finding images for each keyword in a FigJam working board, then organizing the best ones into a gridded presentation; avoid overwhelming boards, boards of "cool designs" unrelated to brand personality, and copying.

## Claims and advice
- [MB][CL] Purpose of a mood board: align everyone on visual direction and style, a source of inspiration, consistency, and faster approval later. (0:00) — opinion
- [TOOLS] Any open-canvas tool works: Figma, Miro (caption "Millerote", verify), Illustrator, Affinity Designer, Canva; he uses FigJam. (0:00, 3:03) — practice
- [P][CL] Before the board, run a sprint with the client team and a personality slider: write down competitors and the client name, place each on scales (elite vs mass appeal, serious vs playful); "not the most scientific" but gets the client talking and shows self-perception. (1:01-2:01) — practice
- [MB] First step is mind mapping keywords, not images. Example keywords: bold and striking, playful yet professional, tactile and layered, where light meets dark, magical, futuristic, empowering, creative, accessible, human-centric. (2:01) — practice
- [MB][REF][TOOLS] Images are found per keyword in Unsplash, Google Images and Cosmos and pasted into FigJam sections (one section per keyword: magic/wonder, energy, "artistic and gradients", playful). (3:03-5:05) — practice
- [MB][VIS-C] Patterns emerge from the images (warm tones, abstract shapes, gradients, purple/teal/yellow/orange; later teal and orange dominant); the client should "feel a bit of an alignment" from seeing it. (4:05, 10:09) — practice
- [MB] Constraints discovered mid-process drive new reference searches (the logo had to work in 3D space, AR/VR, 2D and animated, so he looked for references suggesting movement). (5:05) — practice
- [MB] Uses an acknowledged cliche (abstract 3D blobs) for colour and 3D qualities, not for form; deletes pieces that do not fit (one removed on screen). (4:05) — practice
- [MB] Keywords tune the designer's brain "like in a radio, tuning in to a certain frequency of style". (7:08) — opinion
- [MB][CL] Before meeting the client, turn images into words: a mind map of four main feelings (bold, striking, magical, approachable) with sub-points (friendly yet professional, trustworthy; playful design, artistic energy, aura; lasting impression, wonder, curiosity, nostalgia). The map also lists new search terms when stuck ("energy waves" led to a new Unsplash section). (8:08-10:09) — practice
- [MB][VIS-L] The working board is "a big mess"; do not show it. Move the best images to a layout tool (Affinity Publisher, InDesign, Illustrator) and make a gridded presentation PDF, section by section, with feelings, emotions and colours. Do this even if you do not plan to present. (10:09-12:12) — practice
- [MB][Q] A mood board restricts: if you then pick a clashing typeface (his joke: Comic Sans) it will not feel like the same league; the board gives shared vision on illustration style, concept, type and UI elements. (11:10) — opinion
- [CL] Without a board the client has no visual alignment and will probably reject the design because you did not collaborate; skipping it is "probably the most important step in getting the client to say yes". (0:00, 11:10-12:12) — opinion
- [MB] Mistake 1: board too overwhelming; select best, grid, organize. (12:12) — practice
- [MB] Mistake 2: focus on brand personality, not just cool designs; the "brand personality board" ties every image to a keyword (rebellious, futuristic, empowering). (12:12-13:12) — practice
- [REF] Mistake 3: copying; a board is inspiration, not duplication; copying one detail straight into the design is "just stealing"; look at the images "in totality" for a feeling. (13:12) — opinion
- [MB] Some conflicting styles on a board are normal (flat condensed type next to Scandinavian layout) but not too many; find the sweet spot. (13:12-14:12) — opinion
- [P][CL] Meet the client before the board to understand personality and strategy; here the strategic point is that the brand must stand out from "a normal AI company" since AI is "just the plumbing". (14:12-15:13) — practice

## Techniques and steps
1. Meet client; run a sprint with a personality slider for client and competitors.
2. Mind-map brand keywords (paper or FigJam).
3. Make a section per keyword and fill with images from Unsplash, Google Images, Cosmos.
4. Delete misfits; note patterns (colour, shape, movement).
5. Build a mind map of main moods and sub-points; use it to fetch more images.
6. Move the best images into Affinity Publisher/InDesign; grid them into a presentation PDF by section.
7. Present to the client; use it as the check against typography, illustration and UI choices.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Mild: uses stock sites (Unsplash/Google Images) and even AI imagery as sources, versus Louis Moss's push for curated sources like Are.na. Accepts some conflicting styles on a board. Claims skipping mood boards is the main reason clients say no.

## For 1000 Islands
Use his keyword-first pipeline for the owner: 6-8 feeling words per direction (for example calm, wild, intimate, aspirational, honest), one FigJam/Figma section per word, then cull into a gridded presentation. Run the personality slider with the owner on 3-4 competitor names to expose differences in taste. Keep the working board private and show only the gridded version. Note his warning against copying: borrow colour or feeling, not layouts.
