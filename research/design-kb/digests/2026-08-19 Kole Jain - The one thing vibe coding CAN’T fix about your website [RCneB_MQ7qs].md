## Core thesis
Vibe-coded sites feel soulless because they ignore the audience's world: no images, generic layout, no visual identity tied to the customer. The fix is a human sequence: define the feeling (identity first), Frankenstein a wireframe from reference sites, then layer identity onto the wireframe, then add motion that repeats a brand motif.

## Claims and advice

- [VIS-T] Fonts: keep the existing sans, replace the monospace with a serif "like a printed menu" to add personality. (2:02) — practice
- [MB] Identity pieces: moody imagery, palette from imagery, texture (counter with pots and pans, or a tablet for the software angle), a serif/sans pairing, accent-colored icons for features, a logo mark. "None of this came from a formula": it is what conveys the feeling and suits the brand. (2:02-3:04) — practice/opinion
- [P][REF] "Frankensteining": build a wireframe by combining sections of reference sites: hero from one, scattered-items idea, rotating logo ribbon from Better Stack, big-image how-it-works from Superpowered, full-screen color tabs from Miro with the image bigger and 90% of the text cut, testimonials from Atios (verify) minus lines and blockiness, CTA like Wise with heavy bold type, image background, left-aligned. (3:04-4:04) — practice
- [P] Once the wireframe is set, discard the references; only the structure is kept. (4:04) — practice
- [VIS-L][VIS-C] Per-section recipe for the hero: background from brand color or image, add noise (also helps text contrast), split the headline lines, subtext, rounded buttons spaced at least twice the heading-subtext distance, scattered image assets with large elements below and small above and a safe margin around text, darken the edges to pull focus to the center. (5:04-6:05) — practice

## Techniques and steps
1. Diagnose: does the site visibly belong to its audience (imagery, language, one clear promise)?
2. Build identity: moody hero image, palette pulled from images, accent colors from a reference, serif/sans pair, icon set, texture.
3. Frankenstein a wireframe from 5-6 reference sites; trim copy; then discard the references.
4. Apply identity section by section (background, noise, type, image layering, vignette).
5. Define one repeated motion motif and bookend hero and CTA with it; add small hover details.
6. Swap identities on the same layout to test the feeling.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against the idea that a clean, modern layout with good animation is enough: "soulless" without audience imagery and identity. Against starting from a template: identity first, structure second.

## For 1000 Islands
Close to our Stage 2: start with a feeling image (real Thousand Islands light), pull the palette from the photos, pick a serif/sans pair that matches a printed travel brochure or guide, Frankenstein structure from travel references, then keep the layout stable while testing alternate identities (our shotgun variants). A photo-led site already avoids the "no images" tell. Motion: pick one motif (for example slow scroll reveal or water-like fade) and repeat it, not scattered effects. Noise/vignette to guarantee text contrast over photos.
