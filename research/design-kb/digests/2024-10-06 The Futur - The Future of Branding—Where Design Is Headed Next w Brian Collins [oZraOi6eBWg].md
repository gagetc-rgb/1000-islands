## Core thesis
Brand is differentiation, not logo, type or color; the highest-value design work is finding the one true story of a client's future and expressing it in precise language, then designing to that story. Collins argues classic branding case-study production is dying (ad holding companies moving in, AI making "good-looking images" cheap), so designers should move to "transformation" work that mixes strategy, finance and writing.

## Techniques and steps
1. Look back at your biggest wins and extract the common pattern before changing strategy (5:09-6:12).
2. Run research, interviews, financial/portfolio analysis; find the single reframe that explains what the client really does (42:01-46:04).
3. Write the brand ambition in plain language (what is / must be / could be) and read it aloud to the client; watch for the "tumbler" reaction (49:07-51:11).
4. Present values and one recommended direction, not three safe options (46:04-48:07).
5. Design everything (photography, interaction, environment) from that story.
6. For community events: no name tags, no sales pitch, few fixed sessions, hosts who introduce people (12:18-14:22, 25:36-27:39).

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Against the standard "present three options" habit and against brand work centered on logos, type and design systems. Against conference-style speaker parades. Believes AI commoditizes images, so craft of language and strategy is where value remains. Counter-view to keep: his claims are self-reported and sell his own positioning; much of the "transformation" talk is vague on method.

## For 1000 Islands
Thin for web craft, but two ideas transfer. (1) Before any layout, write the one-sentence story of the Thousand Islands offer (what is, what must be, what could be) and let photography and tone derive from it; this fits our Stage 2 "direction" step. (2) When showing the owner direction options, present one recommended direction with its values (e.g. calm, discovery, unhurried) rather than three "safe" options, and avoid debating shades. Avoid the generic words "bold, relevant, innovative" in copy; a specific line beats an interchangeable one.
