## Core thesis
Most landing pages use a hero visual (blob, isometric scene, random character) that is irrelevant to the copy, which makes the visitor's brain work harder and kills conversion. A key visual must reinforce the headline; abstraction is fine if it is relevant, and a literal image of a person enjoying the outcome is strongest.

## Claims and advice
- [Q][VIS-I] Visitors decide in under 2 seconds whether to buy; an abstract, irrelevant key visual gives them nothing to decide on and they close the tab. (0:00) — opinion, no source given
- [Q] Plot of key visuals: horizontal axis abstract to literal, vertical axis irrelevant to relevant; the best sites sit in the relevant zone, and he claims most analysed landing pages fall in the irrelevant, abstract areas. (1:00) — opinion/evidence claimed from his platform
- [Q][AIS] Pretty-but-random visuals are used "in hope that the aesthetic usability effect will overpower everything else that's wrong"; the brain tries to find a connection between text and image, works harder, and likes the site less. (3:01-5:04) — opinion
- [Q] Good landing page order: define a clear problem many people have, present a solution grounded in reality (say why and how it works, not "AI magic"), then clear doubts and show a benefit or incentive to act. (2:00) — practice
- [VIS-L] Copy structure for Western landing pages: left-aligned or centered; logo top left, a small kicker with social proof, H1 (benefit or curiosity driven), clarifying description, low-friction CTA. (3:01) — practice (note: our project rules ban eyebrow/kicker labels)
- [Q][AIS] Bad copy signs: meaningless "gold standard" kicker, headline that tells the user to "take control" instead of solving the problem, AI word-salad description, generic "Get started" CTA (implies work, offers no benefit). (4:03) — opinion
- [Q] Better copy: use a specific number that does not look rounded (17,308 users, not 17,000), a relatable number-driven claim (636 notifications a day, only 5 matter), and a low-friction CTA ("Start for free"). A number chain (17k, 636, 5) gives the brain a logical path. (5:04-7:06) — practice (example invented by him)
- [Q] Fix copy before making any visuals; it takes iteration and customer research, his demo is only a mental model. (5:04) — practice
- [VIS-I] Rating of 10 hero options for the same product: blob (very abstract, irrelevant), lone hero character, bubbles, isometric scene (looks like serious work but "makes absolutely no sense", can lower conversion), swarm of particles attacking a centre (clear metaphor but nothing filtered), phone swarmed by notifications (literal but only shows the problem), funnel (relevant, but filtered items not highlighted), phone showing three notifications (literal, a bit boring), invisible wall blocking hundreds of notifications with five getting through (best abstract option), a person relaxing and reading a book with the phone on the table showing five notifications (best overall). (8:06-15:15) — opinion
- [VIS-I] Abstraction "is not your enemy" if it is relevant; the abstract wall works because it shows the claim and the number. (14:12) — opinion
- [VIS-I][EMO] Human outcome images (a person relaxed) are predicted to make a comeback in the age of AI: they show an outcome, an emotion, and a direct benefit, and people aspire to be like others doing something; may not suit every business. (14:12-15:15) — opinion
- [BIZ][AIS] Designers should think about the client's bottom line and repeat work, not ship "AI-generated BS visuals"; clients now come for "deslopification" (removing AI feel, abstraction, irrelevance). (15:15-16:17) — opinion, with commercial interest

## Techniques and steps
1. Write the copy first, with a specific problem, a claim grounded in how it works, concrete numbers, and a low-friction CTA.
2. For the hero visual, ask on the chart: is it relevant to the headline, and is it abstract or literal?
3. If abstract, make it show the claim (blocking wall, five getting through); if literal, show the outcome and a person, not only the problem.
4. Check that text and image can be connected in a glance; if the brain must decode the image, change it.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
Goes against the abstract blob, gradient and isometric hero trend; says abstraction can work, which separates him from a blanket "always be literal" stance. Contrasts with award-style sites that lead with mood over message (not named).

## For 1000 Islands
Hero photo must say something about the offer, not just look pretty: show a person in a place doing the thing the trip promises (boat, swim, quiet morning), with the headline and CTA specific ("Plan a private boat day", not "Get started"). For a photo-led site, this is a good test for choosing hero images: does the photo reinforce the headline? Use concrete numbers (islands, days, guests) in copy. The kicker tip conflicts with our house ban; skip it.
