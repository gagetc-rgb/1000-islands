## A. Rules with at least three independent voices

1. ◆◈ **Reduce image size before upload; use a modern format (WebP/AVIF) and a compression tool.** (VG, LS, BF, CH, SY; five independent voices)
   - VG [10:12]: "if those images get uploaded onto a website and they're huge files, I mean, it's not worth having a huge file on a website. It will just slow the website down."
   - LS [10:12]: "lo que estamos buscando es que sea un tamaño menos de 100 kb no es obligatorio" (caption: the target is under 100 KB, "not obligatory").
   - BF [1:00]: "make sure your images are compressed and you likely want to use modern formats like webp"
   - CH [23:32]: a retailer case study: "a 51 percent decrease in image bytes being served down with no noticeable difference so optimize your images people"
   - SY [19:24]: "if we were to put like a proxy in front of that and then compress them, maybe convert to AVIF or WebP, that would probably give us quite a bit"
   - Primary ◆ web.dev: "AVIF is even newer than WebP, only supported in Chrome and Opera since 2020, Firefox in 2021, and Safari in 2022." Primary gives **no numeric budget**; the numbers in the videos are habits (see C).

2. ◆◈ **Give every image `width` and `height` (or a CSS aspect ratio) so the page does not jump while loading.** (CH, BF, SY; three independent voices)
   - CH [3:05]: "always include width and height size attributes on your images and video elements"
   - BF [3:02]: "one of the easiest ways to get bad CLS is to use images without Dimensions when using images they should always have a width and height or alternatively use the CSS aspect ratio property"
   - SY [6:07]: "if you do not have the width and height of an image encoded on your image tag, it's going to cause cumulative layout shift."
   - Primary ◆ web.dev: "Images without dimensions Always include width and height size attributes on your images and video elements."

3. ◈ **Choose or shoot each picture for the layout it will sit in: free space for the headline and enough contrast behind the text.** (VG, RS, FCC weak)
   - VG [15:17]: "on every bottom image of a each page, um, we needed to make sure there was that positive space, whether it was, um, you know, a different colored background, but a person to the left or the right"
   - RS [10:09-11:10]: "make sure that there is a space make sure that there is contrast in the background and make sure that it's kind of a simplified there's not too many details"
   - FCC [16:13] (garbled caption): the demo adds a white translucent colour over the background image and says "Did you see this? Suddenly it became brighter" so the text is readable. Machine-translated, so counted only as a weak third voice.
   - Primary ◆ WCAG 1.4.3: contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text; it applies to the pixels actually behind the text, not to the idea of "enough space". No source gives a design rule beyond that.

4. ◈ **Prefer your own, authentic photos; if you use stock, read the licence of that site.** (RS, LS, VG)
   - RS [4:04-5:05]: "when you usually buy an asset online a lot of people have probably also bought it and it kind of puts you in a place that you know your website might look like somebody else's website"
   - LS [3:03]: "la mejor opción es que tomes tu propias imágenes es lo mejor" (caption: the best option is to take your own images); LS [4:05]: "no se trata de ir a google descargar cualquier imagen y subirla a nuestra web sin ningún permiso" (not just any image from Google)
   - VG [28:29]: "AI for me through imagery, it's it's not authentic." and [29:30]: "You've got to have direction um and and planning" (the host, a photographer, adds that such images are the ones "you can use on websites a lot easier"). Opinion from two people who sell design and photography.
   - Primary ◆ Pexels licence: "All photos and videos on Pexels are free to use." "Attribution is not required." "Identifiable people may not appear in a bad light or in a way that is offensive." Terms differ by site (Pixabay's page was not retrievable: **not verified**).

5. ◆◈ **Give images descriptive file names and alt text.** (LS plus the five authors in `answers/07-accessibility.md`, rule 5)
   - LS [13:16]: "para las personas que tiene y que tiene alguna discapacidad esto les ayuda muchísimo ... es bueno que le pongamos siempre texto alternativo y título" (caption; accessibility for people with disabilities, plus Google ranking)
   - Primary ◆ Google: "When possible, use filenames that are short, but descriptive." and alt text "improves accessibility for people who can't see images". WCAG 1.1.1 (in `answers/07`).

## B. Two voices or one

- ◆◈2 **Hero images are usually the LCP element: optimise them, never lazy-load them, and tell the browser their priority.** CH [19:29]: "as UX patterns have shifted towards us using more hero images in our pages so it's very very important to optimize our images"; CH [25:34]: after the work "previously those hero images were not rendering in a total about 11 seconds in now LCP happens at about 4 seconds"; BF [2:01]: "you can change the fetch priority on images to either high or low so that low priority image is at the bottom of the page won't get loaded before the more important ones". Primary ◆ web.dev: "Never lazy-load your LCP image, as that will always lead to unnecessary resource load delay, and will have a negative impact on LCP." No imagery video says this directly; CH [23:32-24:34] discusses a lazy-loading heuristic that fetched an off-screen image.
- ◆◈2 **Resolution switching is not art direction.** DR [3:06-7:14]: `srcset`/`sizes` serve the same picture at different sizes and the browser chooses; "what is our direction our direction is changing the image on the mobile so we actually have different images not just the same image scaled and for that we have picture" [6:12]. VG [19:19-20:20]: a portrait picture scaled edge to edge makes "that person is going to be huge in the header area ... art direction is really important with a photographer at the very beginning". Primary ◆ MDN: "(resolution switching), or different images suitable for different space allocations (art direction)" and `<picture>`'s "Common use cases ... Art direction." The two speakers describe the same problem from different ends (code vs shoot).
- ◇ **Shoot every set-up in both landscape and portrait.** Host Marcus [20:20]: "it's got to be done in landscape and it's got to be done in portrait mode as well"; [21:20]: "for photographers, I think it's definitely shooting in landscape and portrait for every scenario"; VG agrees ("Yeah, indeed", [21:20]) and describes the failure it prevents (see the art-direction bullet). Her "60/40" mobile share ("I think it's probably 60/40 actually and going up", [20:20]) is a guess, **not verified**; use the site's own analytics.
- ◇ **Let the editor mark the subject (focal point) so crops never cut it.** DR [18:33]: "I don't want to cut the face of a prime minister"; [23:44]: focal point "gives you basic ire Direction basic control and actually it really requires low effort" (caption garble: "ire Direction" = art direction). Primary ◆ MDN `object-fit: cover` crops to fill the box and `object-position` defaults to `50% 50%` (centre), so an off-centre subject can be cut unless the position is set; the 'cut' consequence is my reading of the default, not a quoted sentence.
- ◇ **Lead with one strong image of the thing you sell; curate hard; show images large.** JM [4:03]: "I lead with one of my most powerful and most recognized images"; [7:07]: "each Gallery should be worthy of its own exhibition"; [8:08]: "just make your images as big as possible on your website". VG [26:26]: the header image should show the person "in situ", "it doesn't have to be a static image of that person looking at the camera". Different advice for portfolio vs service sites; not applicable word for word to a tour operator.
- ◇ **Keep the site's own colours out of the photo area.** JM [3:01]: "the main base of your website where your images are shown black background white background maybe gray that's about it". Portfolio convention; not checked against any standard.
- ◇ **Check the picture and the page in five seconds.** VG [21:20-23:22]: "can a stranger tell what you do in 5 seconds"; one clear next step. (UX; see `answers/01-ux-ui.md`.)
- ◇ **Composition techniques (thirds, frames, shapes, leading lines).** SG [1:01-8:12]: practitioner opinion; not about web layout, and every technique is a heuristic. Useful only as vocabulary when briefing a photographer.
- ◇ **Watermark your own photos.** LS [8:10]. No other source mentions it; a watermark over a hero image conflicts with VG's and RS's "clean space for text".
- ◇ **Face the AI question.** VG [28:29]: real, authentic photographs matter more now; a seller's opinion. No source tests whether visitors can tell the difference.

## C. Disagreements

- **File-size targets.** VG ~400 KB at about 2,000 px wide ([10:12-11:13], "it depends on the image"); LS under about 100 KB ([10:12]); CH's case study brought above-the-fold images down to 14.5 KB in total ([24:34]). Different roles (hero vs background vs thumbnails) and different decades of connection speed. No primary page gives a number. Rule for the course: measure the page, not the file (Lighthouse/PageSpeed), and check the largest image.
- **Contact forms.** JM [11:11]: "stop it with the forms"; [12:13]: "just put your name put your email out there no forms". The forms research (`answers/08-forms-conversion.md`) treats a short, clear form as normal for a service business and compatible with showing an email as well. Different audience (editors with direct emails vs travellers who book).
- **Hiding dates on news.** JM [12:13]: "I like to leave the date off". This is a cosmetic trick; it hides freshness from visitors and search engines. Treat as ◇ and do not copy.
- **Blurry backgrounds.** VG likes shallow depth of field for copy space ([14:16]); the host says it "can be seen as being a bit over done" ([14:16-15:17]). Taste.
- **Static portrait vs scene.** VG wants the person in situ; JM leads with the single best image whatever it shows. Not a conflict about facts.
- **Stock vs own.** RS and LS accept stock when budget is tight; both warn about sameness or licence. VG's clients are always briefed for custom shoots. Budget decision.

## D. Notes for the lesson (photography half of lesson 9)

- Checks a non-coder can do on any page: (1) open DevTools, Network, filter Img: sort by size, find the largest image and its dimensions; (2) does every image have `width` and `height` in the HTML (right-click, Inspect)? (3) is the hero image the LCP in PageSpeed Insights and is it NOT marked `loading="lazy"`? (4) at phone width, is the subject of the hero still in frame (crop test) and is the text readable (contrast tool)? (5) who owns the licence for each image?
- Ask the AI: "Export WebP (AVIF if supported) in at most three widths; give every `<img>` width, height, `alt`, and `srcset`+`sizes`; the first-screen image has `fetchpriority="high"` and no `loading="lazy"`, all others `loading="lazy"`; for the hero use `<picture>` with a separate portrait crop for phones; keep text off the busy part of the picture and check 4.5:1 contrast."
- When commissioning photos: brief the photographer for landscape and portrait of each scene, free space at one side for text, and the real place rather than a generic one (VG, RS).
- **Thin and unresolved:** travel/hospitality photography briefs, how many photos a tour page needs, drone/underwater/low-light handling, model releases for identifiable people, how to write alt text for atmospheric destination photos, image CDNs, video hero loops (not covered at all), the speed cost of image sliders, Pixabay and Unsplash licence terms, non-English imagery sources (none found).
