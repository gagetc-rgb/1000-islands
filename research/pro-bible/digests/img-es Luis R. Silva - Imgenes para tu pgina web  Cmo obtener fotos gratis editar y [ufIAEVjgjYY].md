## Core thesis
Take your own photos if you can; otherwise use trustworthy free stock sites after reading the licence; resize and compress every image before upload (target under about 100 KB), give it a descriptive name and alt text, and set the social share image so links on Facebook show the right picture.

## Claims and advice
- [IMG] The best option is to shoot your own images with a phone; stock sites (Pexels, Pixabay) are the second option. (3:03-4:05) — type: opinion
- [IMG][PRIV] Read the "free use" licence terms; do not pull arbitrary images from Google, because it can cause legal problems. (4:05-5:07) — type: practice (legal framing is general; not a legal analysis and not tied to a jurisdiction)
- [IMG] Organise files in folders (originals/stock, edited, web-ready) and delete unused ones. (5:07-6:07, 18:24) — type: practice
- [IMG][PERF] Resize to a standard size before upload (1280x720 or 1920x1080 given as examples) and measure in pixels, not print units; a 462 KB stock photo is "too heavy" for a web page. (6:07-7:09) — type: practice
- [IMG] Add a watermark to your own photos; his reason is proof of ownership and discouraging copying. (7:09-8:10) — type: opinion (watermarks are debated: they can clutter a hero image and are trivially cropped; he applies his own logo on an example and then admits it looks redundant, 14:17)
- [IMG][PERF] Export JPG, then compress with a free tool (Squoosh); he lowers quality until the file is under about 100 KB ("it's not obligatory", 10:12) and checks visually that the picture is not damaged; after Canva and compression the example went from 462 KB to 332 KB to 235 KB ("mucho mejor... que 130 400 o 300", 11:14, caption numbers are noisy). (8:10-11:14) — type: practice (the 100 KB target is a habit, not a standard)
- [SEO][A11Y] Use a descriptive file name and alt text that include relevant words (for instance the place) because it helps Google and people with disabilities. (11:14-13:16) — type: practice
- [SEO][IMG] Set the page's social/SEO title, description and share image in an SEO plugin; the preview shows how the page looks in Google and on Facebook/Twitter. (14:17-16:23) — type: practice
- [IMG][UX] After changing an image, use Facebook's Sharing Debugger to force a fresh scrape ("Debug", "Scrape Again"); then a plain link in a Facebook post shows the new image and a click takes people to the site. (16:23-18:24) — type: practice

## Techniques and steps
1. Shoot or download (check the licence).
2. Resize in Canva to a standard size; add logo if it is your own photo.
3. Export JPG; compress under about 100 KB.
4. Upload with a descriptive name and alt text.
5. Set the share image in the SEO plugin; run the Facebook Sharing Debugger.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
- Vicky Gray (Commercial Photographer): around 400 KB for a 2,000 px image; Silva: under about 100 KB for 1280-1920 px. These differ by a factor of four and apply to different roles (hero vs background). No primary page gives a single number; treat both as habits (◇).
- Watermarking own photos: Silva recommends it; none of the other five imagery sources mention it.
