# What Website Designers Wish Photographers Knew
- Source: Commercial Photographer podcast (YouTube, en; host Marcus, guest Vicky Gray of Blue Mango Creative) · 2026-08-18 · 34 min · https://youtu.be/bhOE0ndV2Gc
- Speaker(s) and why they matter: Vicky Gray, UK Squarespace designer for 26 years, works with pubs, hotels, coaches and consultants, and goes on photo shoots to art-direct them. A practitioner designer talking to photographers; the host is a commercial photographer. Podcast chat, loosely structured, many anecdotes.
- Relevance to course: 4 (lesson 9, photography half: art direction for hero images, text-safe space, orientation) · Freshness matters? medium (the "60/40 mobile" figure is a guess)
- Codes: IMG, UX, UI, PERF, AIW
- Notes: no sponsor segment; the guest promotes her own "branding cards" process (a sales-flavoured method, "no other designer uses it", 3:07). Numbers are spoken from memory and not sourced. UK small-business context, hospitality is mentioned only in passing ("pubs and hotels", 2:04).

## Core thesis
Good website photos are planned, not collected afterwards: designer, photographer and copywriter agree on brand, location, orientation and space for text before the shoot, and the designer supplies web-ready files; the page must say within seconds who it is for and what to do next.

## Claims and advice
- [IMG][PERF] Photographers should deliver both high-res and web-sized versions; a common mistake is uploading unreduced originals, which "will just slow the website down". She says "70 72 dots per inch" and aims for about 400 KB, roughly 2,000 px across. (10:12-11:13) — type: practice. The DPI figure is irrelevant for screens (pixel dimensions matter); the 400 KB target is her habit, not a standard.
- [IMG][UI] Plan "positive space" (the guest's re-wording of negative space) in every shot where copy will sit over the image, e.g. subject left or right with a plain area for text. (15:17-16:17) — type: practice
- [IMG][UI] Break up long pages with images and headings so a scanner can move down the page; "less is more" in overlaid copy. (16:17-17:17) — type: opinion
- [IMG][UX] Orientation: landscape suits site-wide header images, but a portrait file scaled edge to edge makes a person "huge in the header area"; the host says photographers should shoot both landscape and portrait for every scenario because of mobile use, and she agrees ("Yeah, indeed", 21:20); she puts the mobile share at "probably 60/40 actually and going up" (20:20). (19:19-21:20) — type: practice (traffic split is a guess; verify with the site's own analytics)
- [IMG] Art direction at the start of the project avoids problems at the end; she attends shoots and client meetings. (9:12-10:12, 20:20) — type: practice
- [IMG][UI] Keep locations authentic to the business (office, venue, real working scene), and add "a little bit of color popping" from the brand palette into props (notebook, mug) rather than clothing for companies; for solo founders brand colours in clothing are fine. (12:14-13:15) — type: opinion
- [IMG] Shallow depth of field helps text sit on the image, but the host (a photographer) says very blurry backgrounds can look overdone; the background used to be part of the picture ("environmental portrait"). (14:16-15:17) — type: opinion, a minor disagreement between designer and photographer
- [IMG][UI] Black and white and duotone tinting must match the brand mood; she tones images very slightly toward the brand colour. (24:22-25:25) — type: opinion
- [IMG][UX] Hero image: do not always use a static portrait looking at the camera; show the person in situ, talking or working; this takes planning and time to relax the subject, and a free headshot day will not deliver it. (26:26-28:29) — type: opinion/practice
- [UX] A three-minute self-audit: (1) can a stranger tell what you do in five seconds (who you help, what they achieve, why keep reading); (2) does it still sound like you and the clients you want; (3) is the one next step obvious (book a call, download a guide, get in touch), repeated down the page. (21:20-23:22) — type: practice
- [UX] Simple, uncluttered pages: "if a message doesn't come through instantly... people are going to click off"; she cites roughly 2 seconds, vaguely. (17:17-18:19) — type: opinion; the number is not sourced and she hedges it herself
- [AIW][IMG] Real, authentic photos with natural gestures are harder for AI to fake and remain the useful kind of image for websites; static headshots are "very very close" to AI quality. (28:29-30:31) — type: opinion

## Techniques and steps
1. Brief the photographer on brand, mood board, locations, props and where text will sit.
2. Shoot every set-up in landscape and portrait.
3. Receive full-size and web versions; check file size before upload.
4. Audit the live page with the three questions above.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
- Designer (shallow depth of field, copy-friendly) vs photographer host (overdone). Minor, taste.
- Hero as portrait of the person: she says "not always the case"; the host says it is increasingly common for personal brands.

## For the course
Lesson 9 (photography half). Use: (a) commission and brief images with text-safe space, both orientations, web-sized deliveries; (b) hero images show the real place or person in use; (c) the five-second test and one clear next step. Do not use her DPI or 400 KB figures as rules; the web.dev/MDN pages cover the technical side. Hospitality-specific art direction is not covered beyond the mention of pubs and hotels.

## Other
She and the host both stress that the human, authentic image is the value now that AI images exist; this is opinion from two people who sell photography and design.
