# Cookie EuGH Urteil - Cookies zu Werbezwecken und die Einwilligung

- Speaker(s) and why they matter: a German lawyer specialised in social-media, online-marketing and data-protection law (named in the caption as "Tobias Röttgen"; spelling to verify). The firm sells legal advice. Explains the CJEU's Planet49 judgment (1 October 2019) for site owners.

## Core thesis
Cookies and similar technologies that are not technically strictly necessary need the user's prior, active consent; pre-ticked boxes and "consent by continued use" are not valid; this applies to all information stored or read on the device, not only personal data.

## Claims and advice
- [PRIV] Two camps before the ruling: advertisers relied on legitimate interest (GDPR Art 6(1)(f)) with opt-out; data-protection authorities required consent (Art 6(1)(a)). (0:00-2:03) — type: evidence
- [PRIV] Authorities' list of technically necessary cookies: session cookies, shopping basket, login status, language settings, media-player flash cookies, consent-storing (opt-out) cookies. (2:03, 11:14) — type: evidence
- [PRIV] Studies suggest "weit unter fünf Prozent" of people agree to being tracked; so consent-based tracking tools lose most data. (3:04, 25:34) — type: evidence (unnamed studies)
- [PRIV] The CJEU (Planet49) held that non-essential cookies require consent; consent needs an active action; pre-ticked boxes and consent by continued use do not work; the user must be told the cookie duration and which third parties have access. (9:11-10:13) — type: evidence (court ruling, lawyer's summary)
- [PRIV] Consent applies to any information stored or read on the device, whether or not it is personal data. (21:24) — type: evidence
- [PRIV] Social media plugins (like button), YouTube embeds and Google Maps set cookies and need consent too; fingerprinting counts as well; analytics tools with "cookieless" modes (named Matomo) still use fingerprint techniques. (15:18-16:19) — type: opinion/evidence
- [PRIV] Whether pure reach measurement is "technically necessary": the court did not accept economic need; his advice then: pure audience counting could still rest on legitimate interest, but profiling, remarketing and retargeting need consent. (12:15-13:15, 6:06) — type: opinion (lawyer's risk assessment, 2020)
- [PRIV] Practical steps: inventory every tool that sets cookies or uses fingerprinting; remove unused tools; set up a consent banner that really blocks scripts until consent (test it technically); allow withdrawal at any time via a link in the privacy policy and a footer link; update the privacy policy (legal basis = consent, duration, provider names). (23:30-32:41) — type: practice
- [PRIV] Consent banners are not plug and play: the banner and the site code must be integrated and tested. (27:36-28:37) — type: practice
- [PRIV] A fine of 30,000 euros by the Spanish authority for fingerprint tracking without consent is cited. (35:47) — type: evidence (not verified)
- [PRIV] "Datenschutz ist ... das neue Bio": compliance can differentiate a business. (37:47) — type: opinion

## Techniques and steps
1. Inventory tools; delete unused ones.
2. Install a banner that blocks scripts until opt-in; test.
3. Offer a link to change or withdraw consent.
4. Update the privacy notice.
