# The Immutable Rules of UX (Jakob Nielsen Keynote)
- Source: Nielsen Norman Group (YouTube, en; keynote at NN/g's UX Conference, introduced by Kate Moran) · published 2019-02-01, delivered for the group's 20th anniversary (2018) · 39 min · https://youtu.be/OtBeg5eyEHU
- Speaker(s) and why they matter: Dr Jakob Nielsen, co-founder of the Nielsen Norman Group, usability pioneer. A career-long, research-based voice; this keynote is a retrospective on what has not changed. NN/g sells research reports, training and the conference, so it has an institutional interest in promoting testing and its own studies.
- Relevance to course: 4 (lessons 2 and 10: why test with users, scanning behaviour, utility vs usability) · Freshness matters? low for process, high for the AI figures (2018)
- Codes: UX, UI, AIW
- Notes: much of the talk is story-telling (Olympic Message System 1984, the Vasa ship, Greek art). The data points quoted (Microsoft Research page-view analysis, NN/g's satisfaction chart, the voice-assistant study) are the speaker's summaries; I have not located the original publications.

## Core thesis
Principles of good UX are stable: early focus on users, empirical measurement, iterative design, and testing before launch; people read the web ruthlessly, so you have seconds to prove usefulness; both utility and usability must be right; and "UX is people".

## Claims and advice
- [UX] The 1984 Olympic Message System succeeded using three principles: early focus on users, empirical measurement with real users (printed scenarios, then prototypes), and iterative design (about 200 iterations for parts). (3:03-6:08) — type: evidence (historical case told by the speaker)
- [UX] In a study the speaker cites, only 16% of designers and program managers named all three principles and 26% named none. (6:08-7:10) — type: evidence cited (source not given in the video)
- [UX] Test before launch: "your design will be tested by users either before you launch or after you launch"; if a usability test finds nothing wrong, "it proves you did the test wrong". (10:13-13:13) — type: opinion/practice (strongly stated)
- [UX] User-centred beats system-centred; more iterations improve quality; use cheap "discount usability" methods and low-fidelity prototypes early because people get attached to designs they have laboured on. (12:13-13:13) — type: practice
- [UX][UI] Details matter as much as the architecture: one wrong word on a button can sink a good design. (13:13-14:15) — type: practice
- [UX][UI] Design differs from art because it must work; rules about what is noticeable (big, colourful, foreground) are timeless. (14:15-16:18) — type: opinion
- [UX] Surface design (appearance) and deep design (what is communicated) both matter. (16:18-18:20) — type: opinion
- [UX] "Vocabulary inflation": the field keeps renaming old ideas, causing miscommunication; results earn respect, not fancy terms. (18:20-22:24) — type: opinion
- [UX][SEO] Users scan, search and leave quickly because the web is huge and most of it is poor; credibility, trust and clear navigation matter; short-term memory limits must be respected. (22:24-24:27) — type: practice
- [UX][PERF] A cited Microsoft Research analysis of two billion page views: the chance of leaving is very high in the first seconds and the first twenty seconds are key; so be concise. (24:27-25:28) — type: evidence cited (original not checked)
- [UX] Utility (does it do what people need) and usability (can they use it) are separate gaps; his example: only 7% of voice-assistant needs were fulfilled, 34% possible but not done due to usability, 59% beyond the technology. Even with perfect AI, usability limits tasks done. (25:28-31:37) — type: evidence (NN/g study, numbers as given)
- [UX] Satisfaction scores have not risen for years although sites got better, because expectations rise with the whole web; flat satisfaction means you kept up; continuous improvement is required. (31:37-35:43) — type: evidence/opinion (own longitudinal chart)

## Techniques and steps
1. Define users early.
2. Test cheap prototypes with real users.
3. Iterate; test again after the redesign.
4. Check both utility and usability.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
- Todaro's "laws" list offers psychology-based rules without testing; Nielsen insists that only testing with users validates a design. Not a factual conflict, a difference of method.
- A different view on engagement signals appears in the Fireship SEO video (dwell time as ranking); Nielsen's "twenty seconds" point is about user abandonment, not ranking.

## For the course
Lessons 2 and 10: use "test before launch with a few real users", "utility versus usability", "people scan and leave in seconds", and "expectations rise". Treat the cited statistics as secondary sources (original studies not retrieved).
