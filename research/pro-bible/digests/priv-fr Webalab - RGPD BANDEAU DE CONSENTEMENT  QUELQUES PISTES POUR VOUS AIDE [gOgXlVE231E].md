## Core thesis
A consent banner does nothing unless every tag is conditioned on the visitor's choice; first map what fires on your site, then orchestrate tags by consent category through a tag manager or code, and test in developer tools. Free consent tools suit small sites; larger sites need statistics, testing and per-country rules.

## Claims and advice
- [PRIV] The CNIL's tolerance period for the rules ended on 1 April 2021, so a consent banner was becoming necessary for French audiences. (0:00) — type: evidence (not verified here)
- [PRIV] Demo: before any banner, advertising and analytics tags and many cookies fire without consent; after proper setup, in a private window, nothing but the consent cookie is set until a choice is made. (1:01-4:05) — type: evidence
- [PRIV] A banner by itself does nothing; its buttons (accept all, reject all, per-purpose settings) must drive which tags fire. (4:05-5:06) — type: practice
- [PRIV][ANALYTICS] Map every tracker on the site (a CNIL-style "cartographie"); it is easier to condition tags centrally in a tag manager; use the exercise to remove tags you no longer use. (5:06-7:09) — type: practice
- [PRIV] Tags can be triggered from the consent categories the banner pushes to the page (data layer); hard-coded tags need a wrapper that runs only after consent. (7:09-9:12) — type: practice
- [PRIV] Strictly necessary cookies are not conditioned on consent. (9:12) — type: practice
- [PRIV] A consent platform can scan the site, categorise cookies from a database of known ones, offer banner templates, and auto-generate the cookie list for the policy page (the free tier shown). (10:12-16:18) — type: evidence (tool demo)
- [PRIV] The IAB TCF v2 framework supplies a consent string to ad vendors; not needed for a simple site. (16:18-21:26) — type: evidence
- [PRIV][ANALYTICS] Paid tiers add consent-rate statistics, A/B testing of banner wording and layout to raise acceptance, multi-country rules by geolocation. (22:27-25:31) — type: evidence (vendor features)
- [PRIV] For a small site with few tags, a free tool is enough; the loss of data from refusals matters little without ad revenue. (26:32) — type: opinion

## Techniques and steps
1. Open DevTools (Network and Application tabs) on a clean private window: what fires before consent?
2. Condition each tag on the matching category; re-test.
3. Keep the cookie list in the policy generated by scan.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
- A/B testing banners for higher acceptance conflicts with the TechGDPR/EDPB view that nudging is a dark pattern; the video itself gives an A/B example that makes "Reject" aligned with "Accept" (23:28).

## For the course
Lesson 7 (and 5): the "test in a private window what fires before consent" check is a one-minute verification the owner can do; it also shows why a banner and a tag-manager setup must be wired together.
