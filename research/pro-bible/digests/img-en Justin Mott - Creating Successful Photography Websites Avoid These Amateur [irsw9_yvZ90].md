# Creating Successful Photography Websites: Avoid These Amateur Errors
- Source: Justin Mott (YouTube, en) · 2024-01-11 · 15 min · https://youtu.be/irsw9_yvZ90
- Speaker(s) and why they matter: Justin Mott, editorial and commercial photographer based in Vietnam for almost 20 years (hotel and travel work is mentioned, 5:04), revamped his own portfolio site. Opinionated single-author advice for photographers' portfolio sites, not general business sites.
- Relevance to course: 3 (lesson 9, photography half; travel/hospitality-adjacent; only part transfers to a tour-operator site) · Freshness matters? low (portfolio conventions)
- Codes: IMG, UX, UI
- Notes: sells portfolio reviews, classes, presets and a $4.99/month membership (promoted at 9:09-10:11 and 14:14); he says he is not selling them while recommending a separate editor (8:08). Uses Squarespace and says so. No data or standards cited; all claims are his experience.

## Core thesis
A portfolio site should make it instantly clear who the photographer is and what work they want to be hired for, lead with the strongest work in the field they sell, keep the design neutral so the pictures carry it, and avoid forms and padding.

## Claims and advice
- [UX][IMG] Use your real name plus "photographer" as the site name; avoid cute photography puns or initials-only logos, because editors and clients must know at once they are on the right site. (1:01-3:01) — type: opinion
- [UX] State your base location and "available worldwide" if true, and back it with work from there; do not claim what you cannot back up. (3:01-3:50, repeated 9:09) — type: opinion
- [UI][IMG] Keep the site background black, white or grey; do not use coloured backgrounds because they "clash with your color images". (3:50-4:03) — type: opinion (common in photography portfolios; not universal)
- [IMG][UX] Landing page: lead with your single strongest, most recognised image, then a gallery collage of snippets from different bodies of work (three images from each story) rather than whole stories; lead with the type of work you want to be hired for (a wedding photographer should not lead with street photography). (4:03-6:06) — type: opinion/practice
- [IMG] Gallery naming and curation: use clear category names that match the work you sell; each gallery should be "worthy of its own exhibition", not eight pictures from a weekend; a personal project needs real time behind it. (6:06-8:08) — type: opinion
- [IMG][UX] Offer a lightbox so visitors can see the image large and make images "as big as possible"; edit hard to remove redundancy, ideally with another person who has no emotional attachment. (7:07-9:09) — type: opinion/practice
- [UX] Keep the bio short, mostly professional, with clients; do not exaggerate credits ("don't call yourself a frequent contributor" after one assignment); the community is small. (10:11-11:11) — type: opinion
- [FORM][UX] Contact page: no contact form; show name, email, and, if relevant, assistant and gallery representative so people can copy and paste. His reasons: he dislikes forms and does not know "where does that email go... what list did I just join". (11:11-12:13) — type: opinion (contradicts the forms research, see below)
- [UX] A news section only if there is regular news; leave the date off so an inactive stretch is not visible. (12:13-13:14) — type: opinion/practice (the date-hiding advice is a presentation trick, and conflicts with trust/freshness signals)
- [UX][PERF] Optimise for phones, tablets and desktops but prioritise the devices your actual clients use; his clients do not mainly use phones. (13:14-13:31) — type: opinion (audience-specific; tour customers browse on phones)
- [UX] Link only business-related social accounts. (13:31-14:14) — type: opinion

## Techniques and steps
1. Name the site "Name, photographer"; state base and availability.
2. Lead with one strong image and a collage of the best of each series.
3. Build galleries by category; lightbox; hard edit.
4. Short bio; plain contact details; optional news.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
- No contact forms: opposes the usual conversion advice. Photography portfolios have different traffic and intent from a booking funnel. For a travel business, GOV.UK/Baymard-based advice and Silktide treat forms as acceptable when short and clear, and a visible email/WhatsApp alternative is compatible with his view.
- Hiding dates on news: the opposite of a freshness signal for trust. Treat as ◇.

## For the course
Lesson 9 (photography half): useful for "lead with your best image of the thing you sell", "images large, with an option to enlarge", "a hard edit beats a big gallery", and neutral backgrounds around photos. Not about web image technology. His advice is for portfolio sites; for the 1000 Islands travel site the transferable parts are the homepage lead image, clear categories and short bio.
