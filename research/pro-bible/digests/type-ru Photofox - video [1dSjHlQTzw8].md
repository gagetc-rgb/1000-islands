## Core thesis
Build hierarchy with weight and size, choose a few reliable families, check Cyrillic support, pair serif heading with a sans descriptor, and avoid high-contrast serifs in small text.

## Claims and advice
- [TYPE][UI] Decide the one accent word; raise its weight (Regular to Bold) to shift emphasis; add contrast between heading and descriptor by thinner weight or smaller size, not both at once (too much contrast hurts thumbnails). (0:00-4:04) — type: practice
- [TYPE] Do not make the descriptor larger than the main text unless you accept that the accent moves. (4:04-5:06) — type: opinion
- [TYPE] Reliable sans families: Montserrat (supports Cyrillic and Latin; wide), a narrower alternative when space is short, Helvetica (huge range but **no Cyrillic**; relevant only if your market is not Cyrillic). (5:06-10:13) — type: opinion/evidence (Helvetica lacks Cyrillic is verifiable)
- [TYPE] Serif options: Times New Roman works if you set optical kerning; Calisto MT; Palatino; a high-contrast serif (Bodoni-like) works for accents but fails in body text because thin strokes disappear at small sizes. (11:14-15:20) — type: opinion
- [TYPE] Combination: a serif heading with a sans descriptor is safer than the reverse because small serifs "rattle" on screens; keep the descriptor's weight no thinner than the finest stroke of the heading. (16:20-22:25) — type: opinion
- [TYPE][UI] In the Cyrillic alphabet descenders and some letters make good alignment harder; align the baseline or fit text to simple geometric shapes. (19:23-21:25) — type: opinion
- [TYPE] For most corporate/online projects sans-serif suits; serif for status, tradition and print; small serif text on screens turns to mush due to pixels. (22:25-24:28) — type: opinion (contested; modern high-DPI screens)
- [TYPE] For print do not go below 6 pt (4 pt for legal fine print). (24:28) — type: practice (print)

## Techniques and steps
1. Pick the accent; adjust weight.
2. Increase contrast of the rest through size or weight.
3. Pair serif heading and sans descriptor.
