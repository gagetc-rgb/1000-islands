# How to handle hosting for clients - What mistakes to avoid
- Source: Rino de Boer / "Living With Pixels" (YouTube, en) · 2020-05-30 · 17 min · https://youtu.be/sfcGejsltO8
- Speaker(s) and why they matter: Rino de Boer, a freelance WordPress designer and educator; recommends SiteGround and Hostinger and mentions a discount code and links on his own website (13:15-16:18), which suggests affiliate income (not stated). Advice is WordPress-oriented.

- Codes: HOST

## Core thesis
Do not host clients on your own account. Let each client buy and own their domain and hosting, then give you login access; this avoids messy exits, unpaid invoices, access to their email, and extra unpaid work.

## Claims and advice
- [HOST] Domain about $10-15 a year; hosting about $5-10 a month; buy domain and hosting from the same company to avoid connecting two accounts. (0:00-2:03) — type: practice (2020 prices)
- [HOST] Four models exist: one unlimited plan for all clients, a reseller account, one single-site plan per client billed by you, or the client owning everything; only the last is recommended. (2:03-3:04) — type: opinion
- [HOST] Reasons: you must run migrations if either side leaves; clients can go bankrupt and not pay invoices; many clients already have hosting; you would control their email accounts, which is confidential; clients ask for small paid-for-nothing tasks. (3:04-6:07) — type: opinion/practice
- [HOST] Make it simple: tell the client the approximate monthly cost, have them sign up (a call helps), and share logins; if they want to leave they change the password. (6:07-7:08) — type: practice
- [HOST] Hosting for your own portfolio should offer subdomains to host demo sites and to start building before the client has a domain. (8:10-10:11) — type: opinion
- [HOST] Compare renewal prices, not the first-year promotion (about $10 a month typical after renewal); HostGator and Bluehost are owned by the same company and are known for crowded servers; SiteGround recommended on reputation; Hostinger as the cheapest option with a free first-year domain. (10:11-16:18) — type: opinion (vendor-adjacent)

## Techniques and steps
Client signs up; shares login; developer installs; client can change password anytime.

## Tools and resources
- SiteGround, Hostinger, a plugin to export a WordPress site to a zip (10:11)

## Quotes
- "Always let them sign up for their own hosting. Let them pay for their own hosting." (6:07-7:08, near-verbatim)

## Disagreements and contrarian points
- Opposes Bryce Matheson's resale model; agrees on domain ownership by the client.

## For the course
Host lesson: the client should own domain and hosting accounts from day one; ask for registrar and host access via delegate or collaborator roles rather than sharing a master login. Hosting brand recommendations are not reusable.
