## Core thesis
Under EU law non-essential cookies need opt-in consent that is freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous; many banners nudge users with dark patterns and are therefore not compliant. A banner product, even from a reputable vendor, does not make you compliant: the website owner stays responsible.

## Claims and advice
- [PRIV] Cookies in the EU are governed by the ePrivacy Directive (2002, updated 2009) Article 5, which needs transparency and opt-in for cookies not strictly necessary; the GDPR defines what counts as consent. (2:06) — type: evidence (legal summary)
- [PRIV] Consent under GDPR Art 4(11): freely given (so a cookie wall is illegal), specific (no single tick for many purposes), informed (a mere link to the policy is likely not enough), unambiguous (pre-ticked boxes or setting cookies before the choice are illegal). (3:09-4:11) — type: evidence (reading of the law)
- [PRIV] EDPB Cookie Banner Taskforce was set up to align enforcement on deceptive design; the noyb group filed hundreds of complaints. (5:13) — type: evidence
- [PRIV] Dark patterns: false hierarchy (prominent "accept all", reject hidden after paragraphs of text; accept button in bright colour, others blended in), sneaking (options behind a small unformatted link), pre-ticked boxes, treating scrolling as consent. (6:15-8:17) — type: evidence
- [PRIV] Check against Art 4(11) and Art 7 GDPR: enough information, granular choice by category, user ticks it themselves, no misleading nudges. (8:17) — type: practice
- [PRIV] National regulators publish banner guidelines; the German good-practice initiative is cited as useful. (9:20) — type: practice
- [PRIV] Relying on "legitimate interest" for tracking cookies while hiding the objection option is a deceptive pattern. (9:20-10:23) — type: opinion
- [PRIV] Settings must be reachable at any time (for example a floating cookie icon). (11:23) — type: practice
- [PRIV] A banner or consent management platform does not discharge controller obligations; third-party frameworks can increase liability. (11:23-12:25) — type: opinion/legal
- [PRIV] Keep the record of processing up to date. (12:25) — type: practice

## Techniques and steps
1. Do not set non-essential cookies before consent.
2. Make Accept and Reject equally visible; granular choices; no pre-ticked boxes.
3. Provide a way to change choice later.
4. Document which third parties get data.
