## Core thesis
Choose type by mood and project context, test it on real devices, and pair by designer or by superfamily; do not use icon fonts without a fallback.

## Claims and advice
- [TYPE] Start from the project hook: a French company, look at French type designers; a period piece, fonts from the period; otherwise browse the gallery blogs of type services for modern designs. (0:00-2:06) — type: opinion
- [TYPE][PERF] Sans-serif tends to render well on screens, but on old screens and old browsers keep letterforms simple and test; on modern screens a serif such as Georgia can be very readable. (2:06-3:06) — type: opinion (2014)
- [TYPE] Pairing: use one family with many weights; use a superfamily (serif, sans and slab from the same designer); or pair faces drawn by the same designer. (3:06-5:09) — type: practice
- [TYPE] Prefer different faces for headings and body for contrast, heavier serif headings with a sans body, or the reverse. (11:15-12:16) — type: practice
- [TYPE][PERF] Icon fonts have no good fallback: if the web font is not loaded there is nothing there; some mobile browsers (Opera Mini) do not support web fonts; consider SVG with PNG fallback. (7:19-10:14) — type: practice
- [TYPE] Learn from books: Bringhurst, Ellen Lupton (Thinking with Type), Jason Santa Maria. (5:09-7:10) — type: opinion

## Techniques and steps
Pick two or three candidates; test on devices; pair by designer or superfamily.
