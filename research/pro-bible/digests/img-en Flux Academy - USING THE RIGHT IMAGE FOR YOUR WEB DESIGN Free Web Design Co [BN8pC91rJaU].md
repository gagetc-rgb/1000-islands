## Core thesis
Three steps: choose the visual genre (photography, illustration, 3D) to fit the message, choose a good image within it (create or buy), and pick images that leave usable space and contrast for the text that will sit on them.

## Claims and advice
- [IMG][UX] Photography grounds the site in reality and shows people, products and real stories; illustration suits abstract or complex stories and metaphor; 3D combines realistic rendering with scenes impossible in real life. Choose by subject and brand. (0:00-4:04) — type: opinion/practice
- [IMG] Create your own images if budget allows, because stock images are used by many sites and "your website might look like somebody else's website"; otherwise use stock (Shutterstock at "one to five dollars" an image per his memory; Pexels for free). (4:04-8:07) — type: opinion; price spoken from memory, not verified
- [IMG][UI] Pick an image with the layout in mind: sharp and high quality (avoid phone-grainy stock), subject clear, "empty" blurred area where the headline will go, enough contrast behind the text, not too many details. (8:07-11:10) — type: practice; demonstrated in Photoshop with dog photos: a busy shot cannot carry white or black text; a version where he flips the image horizontally gives a blank area on the left for text
- [IMG][UI] Flipping an image can move the free space to where the layout needs it (10:09). — type: practice (only safe if there is no text, logo or asymmetry in the photo)
- [UI] Collect references (Awwwards, Lapa Ninja) and categorise styles to communicate with clients. (2:01, 11:10) — type: practice

## Techniques and steps
1. Decide the genre.
2. Create or buy; check quality.
3. Test the image in the layout: where does the headline go, is the contrast sufficient?

## Tools and resources
- Shutterstock (paid), Pexels (free), Awwwards, Lapa Ninja, Photoshop.

## Quotes
- "make sure that there is a space... make sure that there is contrast in the background and make sure that it's kind of a simplified" (10:09-11:10, caption near-verbatim)

Lesson 9 (photography half). Use the "space for text and contrast" test and the "stock vs own images" trade-off (for a tourism business: own photography of the real islands matters more than for a generic site). Licensing detail is not covered here; see Silva and the licence pages.
