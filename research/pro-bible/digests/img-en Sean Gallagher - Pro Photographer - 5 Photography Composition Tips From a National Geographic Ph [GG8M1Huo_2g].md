## Core thesis
Combine a few composition devices (rule of thirds, frame within a frame, strong shapes, centred subject with a purposeful surrounding, leading lines and diminishing perspective) to control where the viewer looks and what they think.

## Claims and advice
- [IMG] Rule of thirds: place the subject on a third-line intersection (the woman at bottom-left, the boy at top-right) so the frame keeps context and balance. (1:01-3:04) — type: practice (a heuristic, not a law)
- [IMG] Frame within a frame: use doors, windows or the way lines fall to focus the eye on the subject. (3:04-4:06) — type: practice
- [IMG] Strong shapes (circle, diamond, square) pull attention to the intended place. (4:06-5:07) — type: practice
- [IMG] Centred subject is clear but obvious; compensate by what happens around it (shooting through vegetation, a slightly tilted camera). (5:07-6:09) — type: practice
- [IMG] Diminishing perspective and leading lines (rivers, roads) lead the eye through the frame and add depth; combining several techniques is stronger than one. (6:09-8:12) — type: practice
- [IMG] Pick one technique and practise until it becomes automatic. (8:12) — type: opinion

## Techniques and steps
1. Pick a device.
2. Check where the eye goes first.
3. Repeat until instinctive.
