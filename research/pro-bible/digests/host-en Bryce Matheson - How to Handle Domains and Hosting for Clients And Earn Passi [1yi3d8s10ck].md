# How to Handle Domains and Hosting for Clients (And Earn Passive Income too!)
- Source: Bryce Matheson (YouTube, en) · 2020-09-15 · 9 min · https://youtu.be/1yi3d8s10ck
- Speaker(s) and why they matter: Bryce Matheson, a freelance web developer who rents a server and resells hosting (75+ clients, over $1,000 a month, 0:00); he sells a hosting-business course/playlist (5:05). **Strong commercial interest** in the advice he gives.
- Relevance to course: 3 (hosting hand-off; the domain-ownership point is the useful part) · Freshness matters? business model advice is timeless
- Codes: HOST
- Notes: responds to a "Living With Pixels" (Rino de Boer) video that says the opposite about hosting.

## Core thesis
Developers should host clients' sites themselves for recurring income, but clients should own their domain names.

## Claims and advice
- [HOST] A site needs a domain (about $12 a year) and hosting (about $4-5 a month on cheap plans). (1:02-2:03) — type: evidence (2020 prices)
- [HOST] Hosting resale: he rents one $30 server, hosts many sites, charges clients $15 to $75 a month; "one client covers the server". (3:03-5:05) — type: opinion/business
- [HOST] Clients mostly do not understand technical details and want a one-stop shop. (3:03) — type: opinion
- [HOST] Domains: he can mark up only a dollar or two; for clients who do not know what a domain is he buys it and rolls it into the monthly fee. (6:06-7:06) — type: practice
- [HOST] Preferred: the client buys and owns the domain, because they then handle the billing and the domain is "the gateway into their business": if the client leaves angry you do not have to transfer it, and they can re-point it to another server ("they have the keys to the kingdom"). (7:06-8:07) — type: opinion

## Techniques and steps
Client owns domain; developer hosts; client can re-point DNS.

## Tools and resources
- Self-managed rented server (4:04); his YouTube playlist (5:05)

## Quotes
- "they have the keys to the kingdom" (8:07)

## Disagreements and contrarian points
- Disagrees with Rino de Boer (who says clients should also pay for their own hosting); agrees on domain ownership.

## For the course
Host lesson: both authors agree the client should own the domain; he differs on hosting. For a one-off site build, hand-over should leave domain, DNS and hosting in the client's accounts, with the developer as delegate.
