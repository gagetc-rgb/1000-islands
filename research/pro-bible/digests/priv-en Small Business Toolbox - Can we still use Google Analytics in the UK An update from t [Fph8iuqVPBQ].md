# Can we still use Google Analytics in the UK? An update from the ICO!
- Source: Small Business Toolbox (YouTube, en, UK) · 2020-01-17 · 27 min · https://youtu.be/Fph8iuqVPBQ
- Speaker(s) and why they matter: a UK small-business and IT trainer (the name is not in the caption); a non-lawyer with strong opinions who filed a Freedom of Information request with the ICO. Opinionated; the ICO answer he quotes is the primary part.
- Relevance to course: 3 (lesson 7: the cost of consent-gating analytics) · Freshness matters? high: UK rules changed after 2020, so treat as historic
- Codes: PRIV, ANALYTICS
- Notes: no sponsor. Long personal rant between 1:01-3:06, and 25:41-26:42.

## Core thesis
In 2019 the ICO required active consent for analytics cookies on its own site; the creator argues analytics is essential, privacy-light and that consent-gating destroys measurement ("a 90% drop in sessions"), and complains the rules are vague for small businesses.

## Claims and advice
- [PRIV] In July 2019 the ICO's head of technology policy said pre-ticked boxes or default-on sliders cannot be used for non-essential cookies, including web analytics; prior consent is required. (8:11-10:15) — type: evidence (paraphrase of an ICO article)
- [PRIV] The EU's January 2017 ePrivacy proposal said counting visitors would not need consent; he contrasts it with 2019 guidance. (6:09-8:11) — type: evidence (a proposal, not law)
- [PRIV][ANALYTICS] After the ICO switched analytics off by default on its own site, its communications team told him of "approximately 90 percent drop in sessions", i.e. only about 10% or fewer visitors opted in (anecdotal). (13:21-14:23) — type: evidence (anecdote relayed from ICO, not a published statistic)
- [PRIV] The ICO had issued no prosecutions for cookie non-compliance and no fines for it at the time (as of an August 2019 reply); a fine of up to half a million pounds was possible. (17:25-19:28) — type: evidence (2019)
- [PRIV] Google Analytics' IP anonymisation drops the last octet of the IP address. (15:23-16:24) — type: evidence (old UA-era feature; GA4 does not log IP, per Google's page)
- [PRIV] Demo: in an incognito window, a big newspaper site set 121 cookies, rising to 145, before any consent click. (20:29-22:35) — type: evidence (2020 demo)
- [PRIV] His view: analytics is the most essential function of a website; the ICO's approach is disproportionate. (19:28) — type: opinion
- [PRIV] Third-party cookies are being phased out by browsers. (23:39-24:40) — type: evidence (2020 prediction)

## Techniques and steps
Incognito check: open the site in a private window and count cookies without clicking anything.

## Disagreements and contrarian points
- Opposes the regulators' reading (consent for analytics); TechGDPR and the EDPB taskforce say the opposite for Art 5(3). The CNIL accepts an exemption for strictly limited audience measurement (see answers/10).

## For the course
Lesson 7: the one real number on the cost of opt-in: when analytics needs consent only a fraction of visitors are counted, so a client should not expect full-traffic statistics. Treat the exact figure as anecdotal.

## Other
He wishes for simpler rules; UK law and ICO guidance have since changed.
