# 1000 Islands Travel — Strategy Brief

## 1. What we are actually building

This is not just a travel agency website and not merely a catalogue of tours.

We are building a **single commercial system** that should:

- strengthen trust in the brand;
- show people why remote Indonesia is worth travelling to;
- remove the fear that remote travel in Indonesia will be difficult, confusing, unreliable or stressful;
- generate B2B enquiries;
- also capture B2C enquiries;
- gradually become the foundation for a CRM and historical customer database;
- support separate SEO and advertising landing pages without creating multiple disconnected websites.

### Core positioning

> **Remote Indonesia, made easy.**
>
> We take people to extraordinary, difficult-to-reach places in Indonesia while giving them maximum experience with minimum organisational headache.

This is stronger and more specific than simply positioning the company as “luxury travel Indonesia.”

---

## 2. Business priority

### Priority #1 — B2B

The main commercial audience in the first stage:

- travel advisors;
- travel consultants;
- concierge companies;
- boutique travel agencies;
- other sellers of travel who need a reliable local partner in Indonesia.

A strong B2B proposition:

> **Your client remains your client. We handle Indonesia on the ground.**

For B2B, the company is not only selling a trip. It is selling:

- local access;
- destination expertise;
- logistics;
- reliable response and coordination;
- reduced operational headache;
- protection of the advisor-client relationship.

### Priority #2 — B2C

B2C remains active. A traveller may discover Raja Ampat, fall in love with the destination, submit an enquiry and buy directly.

But when allocating time, content and the initial marketing budget:

**B2B first, B2C second.**

---

## 3. Main KPI

The core metric is:

## **Closed deals**

Not pageviews. Not Instagram followers. Not average ticket size.

The funnel should be measured as:

`Visitors → Enquiries → Qualified Leads → Proposals → Closed Deals → Revenue → Gross Profit`

Track B2B and B2C separately, and track acquisition source, for example:

- Advisor outreach
- Google
- Organic search
- Instagram
- Referral
- Returning client
- Other

The current priority is **growth in number of clients**, not aggressive growth in average order value.

---

## 4. Financial target

Business milestone:

**$10,000/month operating profit.**

Historical spend is approximately:

**$2,000–2,500 per person**, with an intention to move toward higher-value trips over time.

The current pricing rule is approximately:

**Total trip cost × 2**

This is roughly equivalent to a 50% gross margin on sales.

Illustratively:

- $20,000 sales → about $10,000 gross margin;
- at $2,000/person → about 10 travellers;
- at $2,500/person → about 8 travellers.

However, this is **not yet $10,000 net profit**, because marketing, salaries, administration, refunds, overhead and other expenses still have to be paid from gross margin.

For now, the financial target should be written as:

> **Milestone: reach $10k/month operating profit, primarily by increasing deal volume rather than maximizing average ticket.**

A proper unit-economics model should be built later.

## 5. Core destinations

The real business priorities now differ somewhat from the earlier draft plan.

### Tier 1 — Raja Ampat

Raja Ampat is the flagship product.

It should receive the largest share of:

- content;
- photography;
- journeys;
- SEO effort;
- advertising;
- reviews;
- guest stories and case studies.

### Tier 2 — North Sulawesi

Instead of selling the destination simply as **Manado**, use a broader umbrella destination.

Recommended structure:

**North Sulawesi**

Inside it:

- Manado
- Bunaken
- Lembeh
- surrounding islands and nature experiences

Possible landing-page title:

> **North Sulawesi: Bunaken, Lembeh & Beyond**

This communicates a much richer travel product than the city name “Manado.”

### Tier 3 — Banda Sea / Spice Islands

Use both the geographical and emotional/historical naming:

> **Banda Sea & Spice Islands**

This works well as a standalone product combining sea, history, remoteness and expedition appeal.

### Later-stage destinations

Keep these in the long-term content architecture, but do not let them dilute the launch:

- Komodo
- Kalimantan
- Sumatra
- Alor
- Sumba
- Halmahera
- others

## 8. Reviews and social proof

The business already has an asset that many new travel companies do not have:

**real previous guests, reviews, photos and trip history.**

Create a structured archive containing:

- client;
- trip;
- date;
- destination;
- photos;
- videos;
- review;
- permission to publish;
- source;
- amount spent;
- party size;
- contact details;
- future interests.

From this database, create richer **Guest Stories** instead of generic testimonial cards.

Example:

### Anna & Mark — 9 Days in Raja Ampat

Include:

- selected photos;
- route;
- a short review;
- key experiences.

This should be much more persuasive than a standalone five-star quote.

## 9. CRM concept

The CRM can become a central operational asset.

Each customer profile should contain:

- client profile;
- trips;
- destinations;
- travelling companions;
- photos;
- videos;
- reviews;
- enquiries;
- amounts spent;
- interests;
- correspondence;
- referral source;
- marketing permission;
- future follow-ups.

The concept can feel like a **private travel-history / Instagram-style page for each client**.

### Face recognition

Face recognition can help sort large photo libraries, but it should not be the core identity system.

Primary organisation should remain:

`Client → Trip → Date → Destination`

AI can suggest likely matches and a human can confirm them.

## 10. Retention and repeat sales

Existing customers should become a separate acquisition channel.

Example follow-up:

> We were organising our Raja Ampat archive and found these photos from your trip...

Send several strong photos from their previous trip.

Then introduce a relevant future journey, for example:

> We are opening several Banda Sea journeys...

This is warmer and more natural than cold advertising.

The same process can be used to request honest reviews from past guests.

---

## 11. B2B outreach engine

The website alone will not create enough customers.

The `/travel-advisors` section should support a separate outbound system:

`Advisor Database → Outreach → B2B Landing Page → Qualification → Call → Proposal → Deal → CRM`

The website acts as proof.

When a travel advisor receives an outreach message and checks the website, they should quickly understand that the company:

- genuinely operates in these destinations;
- has real guests;
- has access to boats and local partners;
- understands difficult logistics;
- looks professional;
- can reliably handle a client in Indonesia.

That is the main commercial job of the website.

## 12. Initial marketing budget — $500/month

Current budget:

**approximately $500/month**, with room to increase once the business starts producing more clients.

Do not spread this across too many channels.

Initial priorities:

1. content;
2. SEO foundation;
3. B2B prospecting and outreach;
4. small paid-traffic / retargeting experiments.

The technical infrastructure should remain cheap relative to marketing spend.

A suitable lightweight stack remains:

- Astro
- Sanity CMS
- Cloudflare
- image CDN
- lightweight forms and CRM integration

A large VPS or complex WordPress infrastructure is unnecessary at this stage.

The CMS should support a workflow such as:

`Human or AI edit → Preview → Approval → Publish`

## 13. Changes to the earlier plan

### Remove Komodo from the launch top three

If the real current business is concentrated around Raja Ampat, North Sulawesi and Banda Sea, launch around those destinations first.

### Elevate Travel Advisors

Do not bury B2B inside a small secondary page.

**Travel Advisors should be one of the main commercial sections of the website.**

### Make real guest proof foundational

Past guests, real photos, reviews and trip stories should be part of the main sales system.

### Build CRM and source tracking from the beginning

Do not treat this as a distant future feature.

### Use Closed Deals as the North Star Metric

The website, marketing and CRM should all ultimately be judged by their ability to generate more closed deals.

### Do not aggressively optimise average ticket yet

Volume growth is the first priority.

### Do not hard-code Australia as the only primary market yet

Australia is a plausible priority market, but it should be validated against:

- inbound visitor statistics;
- spending power;
- flight accessibility;
- search demand;
- B2B advisor-network potential.

If Australia wins on those metrics, then adapt copy, advertising and advisor outreach specifically to Australians.
